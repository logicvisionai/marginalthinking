# Structural Opportunity Atlas

## Purpose

The Structural Opportunity Atlas is a cross-cutting analytical layer of Marginal Thinking. It identifies structural conditions that either block economic activity or create usable economic leverage. It is not a fifth research program and it does not alter the four-program editorial architecture.

The unit of analysis is not a country score. It is a specific structural condition observed from an explicit actor, activity or business-model perspective.

## Four structural states

1. **Blocking inefficiency** — a structural deficiency currently destroys, delays or materially constrains value creation. The existence of a problem is not by itself an opportunity.
2. **Exploitable inefficiency** — a measurable structural gap exists and there is a plausible, accessible mechanism through which an actor can capture value by reducing or working around it.
3. **Blocking efficiency** — an efficient incumbent system, market design or infrastructure compresses margins, removes arbitrage or raises the hurdle for a particular entrant or business model.
4. **Leverageable efficiency** — an existing efficient capability can be used as infrastructure or productive leverage by other actors.

The same structural feature may legitimately occupy different states for different perspectives. Every entry therefore requires a perspective field.

## Required analytical chain

Every public entry must make the following chain legible:

**structural condition → mechanism → affected actor/activity → economic implication → catalyst or constraint → evidence → confidence**

Where a state may be changing, the Atlas also records a transition watch:

**current state → candidate state → observable trigger → invalidation condition**

Transitions are analytically more important than static labels and should receive priority in the daily scan.

## Evidence standard

A public entry requires at least one source sufficiently close to the claim. Primary and official sources are preferred for infrastructure, regulation, system statistics and public programmes. Secondary sources can provide context but cannot carry a material claim when primary evidence is available.

Each entry must contain:
- explicit perspective;
- state;
- mechanism;
- economic implication;
- material constraints or contrary evidence;
- one or more catalysts where applicable;
- horizon;
- confidence;
- last verification date;
- source URLs and source dates.

Do not convert qualitative uncertainty into a false 0–100 precision score.

## Daily, weekly and monthly cadence

### Daily — signal detection
The daily scanner searches for material changes in infrastructure, regulation, capacity, market design, technology deployment, capital access, logistics, energy, labour, trade and institutions. It writes candidates only when a new fact could change a state, catalyst, confidence or transition path.

A headline that does not alter the structural mechanism is not an Atlas signal.

### Weekly — validation
Candidates are compared with the canonical Atlas, checked against primary evidence and either discarded, retained as watch items or promoted into a validated structural change.

### Monthly — reassessment
The monthly pass asks whether accumulated evidence changes the structural classification itself, whether an opportunity has become crowded or inaccessible, and whether an efficiency has become sufficiently mature to act as leverage or a barrier.

## Publication discipline

The Atlas is an analytical discovery system, not an investment recommendation, country ranking or attractiveness score. It must not use the four states as moral judgments about countries or institutions.

Do not publish a state merely to fill a quadrant. Absence of evidence is preferable to synthetic symmetry.

## First-wave design

The initial release deliberately contains paired and transitional cases:
- an efficiency that can be leveraged;
- the same class of efficient infrastructure acting as a barrier to a different revenue model;
- an infrastructure failure that remains blocking;
- a blocking condition moving toward exploitability because access, capital or regulation changed;
- grid constraints where the solution path is observable but not yet complete.

This makes the Atlas a dynamic model of economic structure rather than a catalogue of national weaknesses.
