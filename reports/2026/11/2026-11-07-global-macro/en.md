# Global Macro — Stronger US payrolls lift rate expectations while AI markets remain fractured

**7 November 2026 · Weekend edition, Brasília time · Information cutoff: 00:55 BRT (03:55 UTC).** Equity, exchange-rate, sovereign-yield and oil quotations refer to completed Friday, 6 November trading. US employment describes October, released on 6 November. The weekend had not opened another regular US or Brazilian equity session. All comparisons identify their own measurement periods. [1][2][3][4]

US nonfarm payrolls rose **165,000 in October**, above a market forecast near 65,000; the unemployment rate edged down to **4.1%**. August and September estimates were revised upward by a combined **84,000**. The release eased concern about an immediate employment collapse but strengthened the possibility of prolonged restrictive interest rates. The official US Treasury two-year nominal yield climbed from **4.46% to 4.51%** between 5 and 6 November; the ten-year rose from **4.84% to 4.86%**. The yield reaction does not mean the Federal Reserve had changed its policy rate. [1][2][5]

The US stock-market bounce was selective. The Nasdaq gained **1.09%** and the S&P 500 rose **0.31%** on Friday, whereas the Dow declined **0.46%**; all three still ended the week lower, with Nasdaq down **3.4%**. Brazil's Ibovespa advanced **1.39% to 168,498.45** and ended its week slightly higher. European equities declined and technology-linked Asian markets experienced far larger falls. Brent settled at **US$98.32**, virtually unchanged on the day amid tension between fading war premia and renewed tanker threats. These movements point to differentiated capital-market stress, not one synchronous global cycle. [3][4][6][7][8]

## 2. Jobs: a positive headline with a weaker distribution underneath

The BLS October employment report presented three layers of information. The first is the **165,000** increase in nonfarm payroll employment, compared with the **48,000 average monthly gain during the preceding 12 months**. Second, August's estimated gain was revised from 133,000 to **186,000** and September's from 29,000 to **60,000**. Third, the household survey reported an unemployment rate of **4.1%**, labor-force participation of **61.9%**, and **7.1 million** unemployed. The surveys differ in samples, units of observation and revision methods. [1]

Sectoral gains were concentrated: professional and business services **+70,000**, healthcare **+42,000**, transportation and warehousing **+25,000**, and manufacturing **+19,000**. Manufacturing's reported employment improvement does not establish an economy-wide industrial investment boom; construction and several other categories changed little. A jobs report can therefore indicate continuing demand for workers without proving uniform demand across every industry. [1]

The weaker undercurrent appears in labor utilization. **4.8 million** people worked part-time for economic reasons, up **339,000** in October. Approximately **2.0 million** unemployed had been out of work for at least 27 weeks, accounting for **27.6%** of unemployment. These conditions can coexist with a lower headline unemployment rate because the statistics measure distinct groups and processes. The broad U-6 underutilization rate also reached **7.9%**. [1][13]

Average hourly earnings rose **0.4%** month over month and **3.1%** over twelve months to **US$38.00**. This is nominal labor compensation. Whether workers' purchasing power improved requires an appropriately matched consumer-price deflator; the wage growth rate cannot simply be equated with real wage growth. Hours worked remained **34.4 weekly** on average. The combination of positive hiring, limited average-hour growth and more involuntary part-time employment argues for resilience with qualification rather than a definitive reacceleration of household income. [1]

The economic effect of revisions is instead to alter the assessed trajectory entering October. [1]

## 3. The Fed, Treasury yields and a changed policy probability

The October employment surprise shifted market discussion toward whether the Federal Reserve would raise rather than reduce rates in December. Reuters quoted rates-market estimates putting the implied probability of a quarter-point December increase at approximately **78%**, compared with **70%** before the data. These are market-implied, model-dependent readings, not an FOMC commitment or a statement of a probabilistic forecast issued by the central bank. [5]

The Fed's most recent formal decision was the **28 October hold at 3.75%–4.00%**. The newly observed November 6 Treasury yields were **4.51%** for two years and **4.86%** for ten years. Higher expected short-term policy rates can move the front of the sovereign curve immediately; long yields also incorporate uncertainty about future inflation, growth and compensation for duration. Treasury constant-maturity estimates are interpolated market benchmarks, not the Fed funds target and not necessarily executable quotes for a particular bond. [2][10]

A stronger labor market does not mechanically imply a higher price inflation reading. It can increase consumption and service demand but may also reflect improving labor supply and productivity. The policy question is whether aggregate demand, wage setting and inflation expectations remain inconsistent with the central bank's price objective. With energy costs sensitive to the Iran conflict, economic releases can favor more than one interpretation. The coming US consumer-price and producer-price reports offer a more direct test of current inflation than stock-index returns. [5][14]

```flow
Official payroll growth and positive revisions → Lower immediate recession fear → Reassessment of expected Fed policy and short Treasury yields
Energy disruptions and shipping insurance uncertainty → Delivered fuel and freight costs → Consumer inflation, company margins and policy expectations
AI stock repricing → Equity financing conditions and collateral values → Investment plans only if financing or orders actually change
```

The arrows denote plausible channels, not coefficients estimated from one trading day. Bond yields, delivered energy costs and real capital spending have different time lags, data sources and causal confounders.

## 4. AI and global equities: Friday's rebound did not reverse the weekly drawdown

Friday's US close was a modest recovery rather than a uniform risk-on reversal. The S&P 500 rose **0.31%**, Nasdaq **1.09%**, Russell 2000 **0.89%**, and Dow fell **0.46%**. Over the entire trading week the S&P 500 lost **2.7%**, Nasdaq **3.4%**, Russell 2000 **2.1%**, and Dow **0.9%**. Recovering part of Thursday's technology losses did not restore the value lost in the earlier sell-off. [3]

The concentration of AI-linked equity risk is central to regional transmission. When investors sell semiconductor or data-center growth stocks, Asian foundries, chip-memory manufacturers, equipment producers and highly exposed exchange-traded portfolios can respond even before cancellations of actual equipment orders. The Japanese Nikkei dropped approximately **5.96%** on Friday and South Korea's Kospi approximately **6.02%**. These market moves are evidence of repriced expected profits and funding risks; they are not direct measurements of physical chip output. [6][15]

European equities also fell: the STOXX 600 finished approximately **1% lower at 648.33**, while Germany's DAX dropped about **1.9%**. European insurers and other sectors were affected by company-specific reports as well as the global technology correction; the region's smaller direct index weight in US-style megacap technology should not be mistaken for insulation from corporate credit, business confidence or energy prices. [7]

Investors have reasons to differentiate between an AI-related valuation reset and an outright reduction in productive investment. Equity multiples can collapse when expected future profits or discount rates change, while previously signed data-center leases, electricity contracts and construction commitments remain active. The production channel becomes observable through canceled orders, capex guidance, financing spreads, capital raising and abandoned infrastructure projects. A broad economic contraction requires evidence beyond one sector's market capitalization decline.

## 5. Oil and geopolitics: a small Friday move hides a large weekly adjustment

Brent January futures ended Friday at **US$98.32**, up only **US$0.03** or **0.03%** from Thursday. WTI rose **US$0.31** to **US$90.83**. Reuters described an estimated **17%** weekly Brent decline and **13%** WTI decline as risk premia unwound amid expectations of possible de-escalation. The Friday close nonetheless reflected renewed concern about US–Iran military confrontation and attacks on vessels near the Gulf and Strait of Hormuz. These are simultaneously relevant but not logically inconsistent forces: future supply risk can be lower than a week ago yet remain elevated compared with normal conditions. [4]

One shipping company reported that a seafarer was killed in a November 5 explosion on an oil-products tanker; its preliminary account suggested a missile attack. Such reports have serious human and maritime-security implications, but a vessel manager's account is not an independently established perpetrator identity or measurement of oil production lost. Reuters also cited British maritime security reporting and specialists pointing to additional shipping distance and transshipment constraints. [4]

At least three prices must be separated: the commodity futures settlement, the spot price for a specific crude grade and the delivered price after freight and insurance. Falling futures may coexist with expensive shipping or unusual local grade premia. For importers the delivered cost determines transport and industrial profitability; for exporters the realized selling price must be considered alongside output restrictions and shipping delays. Without documented cargo quantities and route changes, it is unwarranted to infer exact changes in global energy supply from these futures prices alone.

The economic distribution is not symmetric. Energy importers face weaker real purchasing power and potentially higher transport cost, while exporters can gain nominal receipts when production reaches market. Refiners and petrochemical manufacturers respond differently according to grades, contracts and inventories. A persistent shock matters for central banks if it passes through to expectations and underlying inflation, not solely because a headline barrel price moves.

## 6. Brazil: a recovering equity market, stronger dollar and restrictive credit

The Ibovespa closed **6 November at 168,498.45 points**, rising **1.39%**; the B3 reported a **0.34%** rise for the entire week. The commercial dollar increased **0.36%** to approximately **R$5.50**. Brazil's local equity rebound alongside a stronger dollar is not contradictory: foreign-exchange movements depend on international rate differentials, hedging, asset flows and domestic fiscal risk, while the index reflects the prices and weights of major listed firms. [8]

Brazil's Copom had maintained Selic at **13.75%** on 4 November, pausing five consecutive cuts. The US labor report did not dictate that Brazilian decision retroactively. Nevertheless, higher yields in the United States can increase the return required for emerging-market assets and affect local companies needing to borrow, refinance or import machinery. The exchange-rate transmission depends on the currency denomination and duration of liabilities, the degree of hedging and actual prices paid. [8][16]

The domestic risk also depends on fiscal implementation. Expectations about the next Lula administration's budget and spending priorities can influence long-term local rates, but a political announcement and a legally adopted budget are different evidence classes. Stronger commodity exporters' earnings may benefit the index while household purchasing power remains constrained by elevated borrowing costs. The country's status as both a major producer and consumer of energy creates mixed incidence from global oil fluctuations.

For a Brazilian investor, the most informative near-term indicators are not the US Nasdaq alone. IPCA releases, Focus inflation expectations, the local forward interest-rate curve, Treasury funding and measured credit delinquency help determine whether the external shock becomes domestic financial tightening. Equity and currency returns should not be combined into a simple claim that money either entered or left Brazil without actual net capital-flow series.

## 7. Europe and China: inflation persistence meets uneven demand

The **5 November** Bank of England decision lifted Bank Rate to **4.00%**, while its published assessment projected UK inflation around **5% by mid-2027**. The forecast describes a possible future price path under its assumptions, not inflation already recorded in November 2026. In the euro area, Eurostat's October flash HICP estimate was **4.0%** year on year, including **20.8%** energy-price inflation and **2.5%** for the core index excluding energy, food, alcohol and tobacco. Higher energy costs can reduce private demand even as authorities weigh persistent inflation risks. [9][11]

China's official manufacturing PMI for October was **49.3**, below the 50 expansion threshold; new export orders stood at **48.7**. This survey signals the breadth of deterioration reported by businesses, not the percentage decrease in industrial output. The Chinese slowdown in general manufacturing and the US technology-share correction may both affect Asian trade, but one should not infer identical changes in advanced semiconductor manufacturing, consumer exports or domestic construction. Different sectors and countries have different contract structures and policy buffers. [12]

The combined situation poses a policy dilemma: a central bank can face higher external costs but weaker domestic activity. Raising interest rates in response to a temporary supply shock protects price credibility only if expectations and secondary effects justify it; doing so amid weak demand imposes additional financing costs. Conversely, ignoring persistent inflation can force a costlier later adjustment. The observed decisions of the Bank of England, Federal Reserve and Copom are distinct choices made in different domestic conditions.

```map
United States | October jobs +165,000 and a 4.1% unemployment rate; bond yields rise on Friday | Establishment and household surveys are different; December policy remains undecided
Brazil | Ibovespa +1.39% Friday, dollar gains and Selic remains 13.75% | Stock returns, FX and interest rates transmit through different channels
Euro area and United Kingdom | Euro HICP flash inflation 4.0%; Bank of England rate reaches 4.00% | Inflation estimate and monetary policy refer to different geographies and dates
Japan and South Korea | Nikkei and Kospi lose roughly 6% on Friday | Semiconductor equity repricing is not confirmed factory shutdown
China | Official October manufacturing PMI 49.3, export orders 48.7 | Survey diffusion is not a measured percent decline in exports
Persian Gulf and shipping routes | Physical transport danger persists even as Brent falls sharply over the week | Futures prices do not verify shipped barrels or identify attackers
```

Regional exposure is qualitative, not a ranking of national vulnerability. It connects distinct official data and dated market observations without inventing bilateral trade volumes, geographic precision or unobserved capital flows.

## 8. Three competing regimes for the next week

| Scenario and economic mechanism | Evidence that would support it | Evidence that would disconfirm it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Resilient US employment with prolonged high rates.** Jobs and wages sustain spending while sticky inflation extends restrictive policy. | Persistent core CPI, stronger nominal wages, Fed repricing and short Treasury yields | Rapid deceleration of prices and expectations, weaker hours and subsequent hiring |
| **Technology valuation correction without real investment collapse.** Investors reduce earnings multiples but contracted projects proceed. | Maintained capex guidance, actual data-center construction, equipment orders and financing | Project cancellations, lower orders, materially impaired borrowing access |
| **Energy shock with weaker real activity.** Shipping insecurity raises delivered costs while industrial and household demand weaken. | Persistent freight premia, rising delivered fuel prices, contracting PMIs and squeezed margins | Sustained safer maritime traffic, lower landed costs and improving demand |

These are conditional interpretations, not assigned probabilities. More than one may occur: continued US hiring can coexist with weak manufacturing orders in another region, while AI valuations may be volatile without immediate changes in installed productive capacity.

## 9. Calendar, method and principal watchpoints

The next major US inflation data are expected to influence rates pricing ahead of December's Federal Reserve meeting. The scheduled **November US employment report** is to be released on **4 December 2026**. Market commentary on potential December rate changes is not a statement of an actual future decision. Europe faces additional activity data and debate about monetary leadership, while the COP31 climate summit opens in Turkey. Such scheduled events are not yet economic outcomes. [1][14]

An assessment of the next trading week should check the US CPI and producer-price data, changes in weekly jobless claims, movements in market-based inflation compensation and credit spreads, and evidence of corporate project financing. For oil, maritime traffic, ship insurance, refinery purchases and the delivered prices paid by importers offer a more reliable gauge than attributing a physical-supply change to futures alone. In Brazil, inflation, fiscal decisions and the interest-rate curve must be interpreted alongside external dollar and commodity conditions.

The central observed distinction on 7 November is **stronger US hiring versus still-damaged global technology valuations and persistent energy-related inflation risk**. Friday's partial US stock recovery did not erase the weekly losses or Friday's Asian and European falls. The possibility of restrictive policy for longer is not the same as a confirmed global recession; confirmation would require consistent output, employment, credit and capital-spending evidence.
