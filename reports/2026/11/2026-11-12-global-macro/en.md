# Global Macro — AI equities rebound while industry, inflation and oil logistics expose a divided global economy

**12 November 2026 · Early Thursday edition · Information cutoff: 01:29 Brasília time (04:29 UTC).** US and Brazilian equity figures refer to the **completed Wednesday, 11 November** sessions. The US bond market was closed for Veterans Day; **10 November** is the latest completed regular US Treasury-market session used here. Brazil's October IPCA, the euro area's third-quarter GDP flash estimate and related European trade releases are due **later on Thursday 12 November** and are not yet treated as published outcomes. No incomplete Asian Thursday session is labelled a close. [1][4][12][18][19]

Wednesday's recovery in the American stock market occurred without an easing monetary-policy decision. The S&P 500 gained **1.31%** to **7,619.38**, the Nasdaq Composite rose about **1.60%** to **25,466.19**, the Dow advanced about **0.49%** to **52,486.49**, and the Russell 2000 added around **0.9%** to **2,846.26**. CoreWeave's 24.7% share-price gain helped reprice expectations for AI computing infrastructure. In Brazil, the Ibovespa rose **0.42%** to **172,060.43**, despite losses in Petrobras, as investors evaluated a possible fiscal adjustment. But the underlying activity data deteriorated: September Brazilian services slipped **0.1%** from August, manufacturing production fell **1.3%**, and São Paulo industrial output contracted **3.8%** on the same adjusted monthly comparison. In China, October consumer inflation was only **0.7% year on year**, while industrial producer prices rose **5.7%**. Asset prices, productive activity and sector-specific inflation are moving in different directions. [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9]

## 2. AI infrastructure: new revenue expectations confront financing and execution risk

CoreWeave's quarterly announcement provided a concrete market catalyst. Reuters and AP reported a **24.7%** increase in its shares after stronger-than-expected results and an improvement in its revenue guidance. AP described roughly **US$9 billion** in anticipated customer prepayments and deposits linked to future commitments, alongside a company expectation of adjusted profitability in the **first half of 2028**. These are different accounting and economic objects: forecast customer advances are not necessarily cash already collected, adjusted future profitability is not net income currently earned, and equity appreciation is not fresh primary capital raised by the issuer. [2][3]

The revaluation spread across semiconductor firms, computing infrastructure and power-system suppliers. AI cloud demand connects scarce advanced chips, networking equipment, substations, power generation, cooling, construction labour and long-term customer contracts. The economic benefit depends on utilization and pricing of installed capacity, not simply how much is spent building it. If a data centre is financed with debt at higher rates, stronger future revenue may coexist with lower project net present value as discount rates rise.

A capacity expansion also generates different effects for different countries. American software and cloud companies may contract equipment from Asian manufacturers; utilities may earn more through additional electricity supply; construction firms receive project revenue before the computing facility reaches steady-state utilization. A supplier's export volume can rise before the cloud operator's customer margins become sustainable. The same cycle creates exposure to overbuilding when expected inference demand, customer retention, equipment useful lives or revenue per unit of computation disappoint.

The previous day's higher-than-expected US CPI still matters. The 10 November official two-year Treasury yield was **4.73%**, up from **4.56%** on 9 November, while the ten-year was **4.98%**, up from **4.88%**. Wednesday's bond holiday means no new comparable daily US yield measurement verifies relief in that discount-rate shock. Stocks can rally during a bond closure, but the price of capital remains a separate unsettled constraint. [11][12]

## 3. Brazil: market relief, unapproved fiscal adjustment and measured economic slowdown

B3's Wednesday closing figure was **172,060.43 points**, a **0.42%** advance, with around **R$28.50 billion** traded. The commercial dollar declined approximately **0.43%** to **R$5.56**. These figures differ from some market-summary sites posting 172,201 or different dollar quotes; the B3 primary-market close and its stated FX reference establish the consistent dashboard. Shares of Petrobras were weaker even as the index rose, evidence that its sector weights and constituents did not react uniformly. [4]

The government discussed a fiscal proposal with the legislature that officials estimated could generate **R$50–60 billion of fiscal effect in 2027**; the B3 account cited Planning Minister Bruno Moretti. This is a prospective plan, not a voted appropriation, cash flow already delivered or independently costed permanent savings. Legislative design, primary versus gross budget effects, recurring versus one-off revenue and the possibility of offsetting spending determine the realized effect on federal financing needs. A market can immediately reduce its risk premium on news that has not yet become law, only to reverse the movement if the legal details disappoint. [4]

Economic activity indicators temper the favorable index close. The IBGE's September services survey showed **-0.1% month on month** and **-0.1% year on year**, breaking a run of **29 positive year-over-year readings**. In such small monthly changes, the IBGE treats the outcome as broadly stable, but the deterioration in the annual comparison is information about momentum. Transport and information/communication activities were among the adverse contributors; these sectors affect businesses whose revenues depend on freight flows, consumer mobility, data services and enterprise spending. September figures describe a past month, not activity already measured in November. [5]

The national industrial production index fell **0.8% in September from August**, while manufacturing dropped **1.3%** and extractive industries increased **1.7%**. São Paulo, the country's largest industrial state, fell **3.8%** on the adjusted monthly measure; Goiás fell **4.1%** and Pernambuco **3.0%**, whereas Mato Grosso rose **4.9%** and Amazonas **4.7%**. These heterogeneous results are more consistent with sector- and region-specific constraints than with a single Brazilian business cycle acting at equal intensity everywhere. The annual national industrial decline was **2.5%**, a distinct year-on-year statistic. [6][7]

The coexistence of weak physical activity and a rising stock index is not anomalous. Prices discount expected future profitability and the possibility of fiscal changes; official output surveys measure already completed production. With the Selic target at **13.75%**, funding equipment and inventory remains expensive. Monetary policy can suppress construction and durable goods before it fully reduces inflation caused by oil, supply constraints or the exchange rate. The Copom's recently published minutes recognized a more pronounced deceleration in cyclical sectors while maintaining the need for restrictive policy. [4][5][6][7][14]

Brazil's October IPCA is due later on Thursday. The next inflation observation could alter expectations, but it cannot be backdated to this early-morning cutoff. [19]

## 4. China: consumer inflation and factory inflation diverge

China's National Bureau of Statistics reported **October CPI inflation of 0.7% year on year**, while the month-on-month consumer index was unchanged. Food prices fell **2.9% annually** and non-food rose **1.5%**. A broad headline CPI figure therefore hides divergent components: pork fell **8.6% year on year**, while the transportation-and-communication CPI category increased **5.5%**. Such variation changes household welfare depending on spending patterns rather than applying a uniform cost shock. [8]

At the industrial level, the NBS reported **producer prices 5.7% higher** than a year before and **0.7% higher** than September. Prices paid by industrial producers for purchased inputs increased **7.9% year on year**. The gap between the consumer CPI and the industrial-gate PPI is not evidence of a mathematical inconsistency: the series cover different goods, stages of production, pricing mechanisms and weights. Cost pressures need not be fully passed to households when competition, inventories or weak final demand limit markups. Some firms instead absorb the difference in margins. [9]

China's recently released October merchandise exports were approximately **35.2% above October 2025** in nominal US dollars, and the January–October trade surplus exceeded **US$1.04 trillion**. Export-oriented factories can receive strong foreign orders while domestic household demand remains comparatively restrained. The official manufacturing PMI, reported for October at **49.3**, was below the diffusion threshold of 50. That number signals more unfavorable than favorable business responses, not a 49.3% reduction of actual output. [20]

Externally generated demand for electronics and AI infrastructure may be a source of industrial revenue while simultaneously exposing China to tariffs, procurement controls, export technology restrictions and slower consumption in the United States and Europe. Cheap imported crude could lower factory input costs, but exchange rates and physical freight contracts mediate the benefit. The main risk is a combination of rising intermediate costs, competitive final-market prices and the need to sustain enough orders to preserve employment.

## 5. Oil: freight normalization can matter more than a daily benchmark quote

Brent recovered about **1.4% to roughly US$92.76 per barrel** in the B3's 11 November market account after two declining sessions. A futures price reflects expectations, inventory, financing and geopolitical risk. It is not identical to the price of delivered crude after freight, insurance, port fees and crude-quality premiums or discounts. [4]

Reuters documented a more granular development: Asian refiners increased their interest in African and Brazilian oil as tanker freight rates declined approximately **50% from October peaks** in several vessel categories. The estimated reduction in delivered freight costs for certain African and Brazilian cargoes to China was about **US$5–10 per barrel**. Reuters reported a Congolese Djeno transaction at a discount near **US$22 per barrel to dated Brent** and Brazilian Tupi crude premiums moderating from almost **US$20** to around **US$7–8**. These are transaction- or corridor-specific market reports, not universally available prices for all cargoes, qualities or destinations. [10]

The delivered cost can fall while the general Brent futures quote rises: a US$6 per-barrel freight saving can offset a US$1.20 benchmark increase for an otherwise comparable cargo, though the precise outcome depends on quality adjustment, freight contract and timing. That is an illustrative difference of two **observed price components**, not a claim that every shipment saved exactly US$4.80. Distinguishing freight, premium and benchmark reduces the temptation to interpret an oil market exclusively through headline futures.

The real economy also distinguishes who earns the income. Lower freight rates may reduce revenue for tanker owners and charter operators while improving refinery margins and reducing input prices for oil importers. Brazilian producers can gain additional Asian destinations yet receive different netbacks depending on loading location, tanker availability, crude grade and contract terms. Such transactions alter bargaining power along shipping routes even when the global oil futures price barely changes.

The EIA's 10 November Short-Term Energy Outlook estimated a **US$124 per barrel October average Brent price**, based on conditions preceding the latest short-run easing, and projected annual averages of **US$97 in 2026** and **US$86 in 2027**. These are different measurement windows: October historical average, annual forecasts, and Wednesday benchmark quote. Their differences do not by themselves prove a forecasting error; later EIA revisions will reveal how delivered-market normalization changes the forecast path. [17]

```flow
Improved tanker availability → lower freight/insurance cost per delivered barrel → more competitive African and Brazilian cargoes for Asian refiners → possible reduction in refinery feedstock cost → potential downstream fuel-price relief
Persistent US inflation → tighter expected Fed path → expensive dollar funding and imported equipment → pressure on emerging-market capital expenditure even while selected AI equities recover
```

The first pathway requires actual delivery and price pass-through; the second depends on future central-bank decisions, credit spreads and firm balance sheets.

## 6. Europe and India: uneven transmission into consumers and capital markets

Eurostat's September retail-volume index fell **0.7% month on month** in the euro area and **0.6%** in the European Union. The euro-area year-on-year change was still **+0.4%**. Both observations can be true because the denominators are August 2026 and September 2025, respectively. In September, sales of non-food goods excluding automotive fuel fell **1.2%** from August, while specialized motor-fuel sales rose **1.0%**. That pattern indicates differentiated consumer behavior, not a uniform collapse in spending. [15]

The euro area's flash October HICP rose **4.0% year on year**. Importantly, the September real retail-volume result and October inflation estimate represent different concepts and months. A price-index increase reduces purchasing power only in combination with wage, tax and household balance-sheet dynamics; a volume decrease by itself does not identify the source of weak demand. [16]

The Bank of England raised its policy rate to **4.00%** in November, facing energy-sensitive inflation pressure. UK inflation and policy differ from euro-area harmonized data, and a single crude quote cannot determine the costs of electricity, natural gas and long-term industrial contracts. Europe's preliminary third-quarter GDP and employment estimate and September goods-trade data are scheduled for 12 November, **after** this cutoff. Their realization could clarify whether weak retail data are associated with a broader production slowdown. [18][22]

India illustrates the other side of capital flows. Reuters reported the Nifty 50 up **0.84% to 25,255.05** and the Sensex up **0.95% to 81,879.4** at Wednesday's close. Foreign investors had purchased almost **US$6 billion** of Indian stocks so far in November according to the report's cited flow estimates. Fund or portfolio investment is not direct productive investment, and country-level market returns are not evidence of new factory ownership. Lower imported crude may support India through its energy trade balance, but the full effect depends on the rupee, taxation and refinery costs. [21]

## 7. Liquidity, capital formation and why the equity rebound cannot be called broad recovery

The November reversal of AI shares is an unusually clear example of the difference between financial valuation and real investment. If an existing share rises 24.7%, its market value rises for the holder; the operating company receives money only in a primary issuance or another capital transaction. A customer prepayment, when received, can support liquidity but may create future service obligations rather than unencumbered profit. A credit facility raises financing and liabilities, while a data-centre construction expense purchases productive capacity and creates a need for profitable utilization.

The Investment Company Institute's early-November US money-market asset stock exceeded **US$8 trillion**. A large liquidity stock is compatible with purchases of AI stocks by another investor group. No one can infer aggregate net new investment from summing money-market positions, market capitalization changes and newspaper estimates of prospective customer commitments. A financial economy can sustain rising valuations while manufacturing surveys or services volumes weaken. [23]

Higher expected US policy rates pose an additional asymmetry. Profitable firms with cash can finance investment internally; leveraged cloud operators or smaller Brazilian industrial firms face refinancing rates, guarantees and interest coverage requirements. In emerging markets, borrowers with dollar liabilities but local-currency revenue also face exchange-rate mismatches. A modest Brazilian currency rebound on Wednesday does not erase the prior week's increase in dollar financing risk.

The differential behavior of Petrobras, banks, consumer shares and technology stocks helps explain why national indexes cannot be treated as a single firm's outlook. A positive index return can coexist with reduced earnings expectations for selected commodity producers and improving expectations for domestic retailers, even before the corresponding economic activity materializes.

The interdependence is not symmetric. American AI investment creates orders for Asian equipment but may require years of monetization; China exports goods into markets affected by higher borrowing costs; Brazil exports commodities while domestic industry and services weaken; Europe faces energy costs and slowing retail volumes; Indian refiners gain from cheaper delivered oil while oil transporters can lose freight revenue. The same event changes costs, revenues, balance sheets and investment incentives at different points in the chain.

## 9. Four testable scenarios for the coming releases

**Risk-asset recovery despite restrictive interest rates.** AI earnings news and customer commitments could support share valuations if financing is available and future utilization improves. It would be weakened by more expensive project financing, cancellations, declining customer deposits or renewed equity selling. The absence of a Wednesday US Treasury close must not be mistaken for bond-market agreement with the rally.

**Brazilian fiscal credibility offsets part of a domestic slowdown.** If a detailed, enforceable and recurring fiscal package is approved, lower sovereign risk premiums could partly ease financing conditions. But September manufacturing and services weakness may persist if the Selic remains high and real household demand softens. Delays, nonrecurring fiscal measures, budget offsets or rising inflation expectations would weaken the credibility channel.

**China's factory inflation compresses margins before consumer prices adjust.** A sustained gap between input-price inflation and restrained household CPI can pressure industrial margins, encouraging export reliance and price competition. Evidence against margin compression would include strong real domestic orders, improved margins, durable higher final prices or cheaper inputs.

**Physical oil freight relief contains imported inflation without removing the geopolitical threat.** Sustained lower ship costs and verified deliveries could make Atlantic Basin crude more competitive in Asia and lower effective refinery costs. Rising insurance premia, renewed disruptions, narrow vessel availability or higher delivered cargo prices would contradict the improvement.

**Observation calendar:** Brazil's **October IPCA and INPC** and Eurostat's **third-quarter GDP/employment flash and international goods trade** are scheduled for **12 November**, after this cutoff. US October **PPI** is scheduled for **13 November**. A new measured outcome can change the assessment; none of those future figures is included as already released. [18][19]
