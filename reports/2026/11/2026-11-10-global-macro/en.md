# Global Macro — Oil de-escalation lifts equities while inflation and China's trade imbalance remain unresolved

**10 November 2026 · Cutoff 01:44 Brasília time / 04:44 UTC.** US and Brazilian closing levels are from Monday, 9 November. Asian equity and oil quotes from Tuesday are intraday, not completed closes. October US CPI and Brazil's Copom minutes are still future releases at this cutoff. [1][4][5][8][13]

The market entered Tuesday with unusually divergent signals. Brent had dropped approximately 5.8% on Monday following US statements suggesting a pause in further strikes against Iran. The S&P 500 gained 2.35%, the Nasdaq 2.74% and the Russell 2000 about 3.15% despite weakness in Nvidia. Early Tuesday, South Korea's Kospi rose more than 5%, Japan's Nikkei about 3% and a broad MSCI Asia-Pacific benchmark 1.5%. Brent was around US$91.56 per barrel, about 1% lower in that Asian session. China also released October trade figures: exports climbed 35.2% year on year and the accumulated January–October goods surplus reached US$1.04 trillion. None of those observations independently proves a lasting ceasefire, disinflation or balanced global growth. [1][2][4]

## 2. Energy: financial relief is not an established physical normalisation

Reuters attributed the overnight easing of oil to President Donald Trump's signal that additional large US attacks on Iran would be held back and to hopes for negotiations. The change reduces the probability investors assign to future interruption, which can lower a futures risk premium immediately. It does not independently demonstrate that ships resumed passage through every threatened route, that insurance has become cheaper for a particular tanker, or that any damaged facility returned to full output. Political signals and measurable logistics are different evidence categories. [1]

Oil-importing households and firms benefit if lower international quotations reach domestic fuel, power and freight bills. Producers, exporters and resource-dependent budgets can experience the opposite income effect, subject to currency, taxes, price formulas and hedge positions. An oil-price decline can therefore raise expected global demand in stock markets while lowering the expected earnings of selected energy producers. The size and speed of pass-through depend on fuel inventories and commercial contracts.

The latest Chinese customs data provide a physical counterpoint. October crude imports amounted to 41.94 million metric tons, approximately 9.88 million barrels per day. Daily imports were 9.4% above September yet 13.3% below October 2025. These references have different denominators and are compatible. Refined-product exports decreased from 7.16 million tons in September to 5.18 million in October, a separate measure that should not be combined with crude purchases. [3]

```flow
US-Iran de-escalation signal → lower expected disruption risk → reduced oil futures premium → potentially cheaper imported fuel → lower prospective headline inflation → some household purchasing-power relief
Failed talks or increased maritime insurance costs → more expensive delivered energy → higher transport and input prices → stickier inflation → more restrictive interest-rate expectations
```

The first chain requires physical price pass-through; the second requires verified escalation. A one-day futures movement cannot establish completion of either transmission pathway.

## 3. Federal Reserve and US households: the CPI test is still ahead

US Treasury yields moved in the opposite direction from oil. The Treasury's two-year nominal par yield increased from 4.51% on Friday 6 November to 4.56% on Monday 9 November. Its ten-year yield rose from 4.86% to 4.88%, and the thirty-year was 5.26% on Monday. Greater pressure on short maturities is consistent with more restrictive expected policy, but Treasury supply, collateral demand and term premiums mean yield changes are not a pure measurement of FOMC probabilities. [6]

The FOMC maintained the policy target at 3.75–4.00% on 28 October by a 10–2 vote; two participants preferred a 25-basis-point increase. October payroll employment subsequently increased by 165,000 and unemployment remained close to 4.1%. That is more employment growth than the 48,000 monthly average of the preceding twelve months. The gains were not evenly distributed: professional and business services added 70,000, healthcare 42,000, and transportation and warehousing 25,000. Establishment payroll data and household unemployment statistics rely on distinct surveys, and earlier months are revised. [7][9]

At 08:30 Eastern on 10 November, the BLS is scheduled to release October CPI and real earnings. That is 10:30 in Brasília. A Reuters economist poll expected roughly 0.3% monthly headline CPI and 0.2% core CPI; neither number is a released observation. Reuters cited an 84% derivatives-implied probability of a December Fed increase, also subject to rapid repricing. Lower oil may improve headline prices more quickly than persistent services, housing and wage-related costs. A favorable core surprise would be different evidence from a gasoline-led decline alone. [1][8]

## 4. China: exports finance growth but deepen external dependence

The October Chinese customs report showed nominal dollar exports up 35.2% and imports up 41.6% from the same month of 2025. The January–October goods surplus of US$1.04 trillion is a ten-month flow of merchandise trade, not the government's budget surplus, China's reserve stock or an estimate of household prosperity. Strong imports may indicate demand for industrial inputs, equipment and oil rather than a broad-based consumer rebound. [2]

Investment in AI computing systems raises demand for electronics and their production chain. Chinese exporters can grow through this external channel while domestic conditions remain weak. October's official manufacturing PMI was 49.3, production 49.8 and new orders 49.4, below the diffusion threshold of 50. These values do not mean output fell 49%: they summarise the direction of responses across firms. Export expansion can coexist with subdued local orders and balance-sheet weakness in property-related sectors. [2][16]

China also announced an extension of its US trade truce, reducing an imminent tariff deadline without eliminating European trade disputes or restrictions on strategic technologies. Companies may accelerate shipments before anticipated restrictions, supporting one month's numbers at the expense of future months. The hypothesis requires future customs and order evidence before attributing a measured portion of the surge to front-loading.

## 5. Brazil: high share prices do not remove high borrowing costs

B3 reported the Ibovespa at 172,236.05 on 9 November, up 2.22%. Dollar quotations were still near R$5.50. The equity move may reflect global appetite for risk, domestic earning expectations and lower oil costs, while the currency continues to reflect interest differentials, political risk and trade flows. The verified B3 closing index should not be replaced by secondary reports giving inconsistent levels. [5]

The 9 November Focus survey, collecting responses through 6 November, placed median 2026 IPCA expectations at 5.22%, 2027 IPCA at 4.90%, 2026 GDP growth at 1.75%, and 2027 growth at only 1.15%. The predicted year-end Selic was 13.75% in 2026 and 12.50% in 2027. These medians aggregate private forecasters, not the central bank's own official policy commitments. A projected 2027 result is not a realised 2026 observation. [10][11]

Lower crude prices can cut expected diesel and freight costs for agriculture and commerce, while decreasing potential oil-producer receipts and some royalties. Exchange-rate changes may offset part of any benefit to importers. High domestic financing rates continue to constrain construction, equipment investment and working capital, and a one-day equity rise cannot identify the direction of lending to smaller firms.

Brazil's Copom minutes are scheduled for Tuesday 10 November. The October IPCA itself is due Thursday 12 November according to the IBGE calendar. Minutes explain policymakers' reasoning for an existing decision; the upcoming price index supplies newly measured inflation. Neither document had been released by the 01:44 BRT cutoff. [12][13]

## 6. Europe: lower spot oil arrives after a large accumulated price shock

Eurostat estimated annual euro-area HICP at 4.0% for October, from 3.8% in September. Its energy component increased 20.8% year on year. A one-day reduction in crude futures cannot erase energy inflation already measured in consumer bills or contracted fuel prices. Gas markets, electricity generation, retail regulation and contract-reset schedules complicate the connection between Brent and European living costs. [15]

The Bank of England raised Bank Rate by 25 basis points to 4.00% at its November meeting by an 8–1 vote. The Bank cited UK September CPI inflation of 3.5% and a conditional projection near 5% by mid-2027. The projection is not an achieved statistic. It also cannot be directly equated to the euro-area's harmonised consumer-price index because geographic coverage and the specific readings differ. [14]

Cheaper imported oil may improve European companies' margins and external purchasing power, yet firms still face borrowing costs, uncertain export demand and accumulated energy tariffs. Energy-intensive plants will not necessarily restart because a single international crude benchmark moves lower.

## 7. Capital, liquidity and the AI-investment constraint

A stock-market rally creates a change in the valuation of outstanding securities; it is not equivalent to new cash paid to the issuing firms. Investment fund net subscriptions are different transactions again. Reuters Lipper data for the week through early November showed large money-market inflows alongside investments into equity and bond funds. Separately, the ICI recorded more than US$8 trillion of US money-market-fund assets at 4 November. These sources have different geographical coverage and may overlap, so their stocks and flows must not be added together. [17][18]

Liquidity preference and a broad one-day stock advance can occur simultaneously because investors have different horizons and because one-day prices do not identify net primary issuance. In private credit, the 4.56% two-year Treasury yield is only a starting reference. Corporate borrowing includes spreads, collateral, maturities and project execution risks. Data centers, power infrastructure and industrial equipment consume capital well before revenues are realised; rising financing costs can weaken their economics even while AI-related exports expand.

## 8. Geographic channels and unequal effects

```map
United States | Completed broad equity rally and higher short Treasury yields | October CPI not yet released
Persian Gulf | Prospective de-escalation reduces oil risk premium | Diplomacy is not verified restored shipping capacity
China | Merchandise surplus US$1.04 trillion Jan–Oct and strong October nominal trade | Domestic PMI remains below 50
Brazil | Ibovespa 172,236.05 and weak 2027 Focus growth forecast | Stock price is not a measure of new credit
Euro area | Flash annual HICP 4.0%, energy price shock persists | Retail price adjustment trails futures
United Kingdom | Bank Rate increased to 4.00% | Conditional UK inflation forecast differs from realised data
```

East Asian electronics feed overseas computing investment; Gulf oil and shipping security affect the input costs of importers; Europe and Brazil respond through energy, capital costs and exchange rates. The same fall in Brent can benefit an importer while reducing an exporter's fiscal income. The same strong dollar-based Chinese trade figures can sustain manufacturing while creating pressure for tariffs abroad. An observed global linkage does not imply identical national outcomes.

## 9. Scenarios and evidence that could invalidate them

**Energy relief with persistent core inflation.** If diplomatic signals become safe maritime operations and lower final fuel prices, headline inflation may moderate while services remain firm. Evidence against this scenario would include rising insured freight costs, fresh attacks or stronger-than-forecast core CPI.

**Re-escalation.** Failed negotiations and impaired transit could restore oil's geopolitical premium, increasing transport costs and reducing importers' real incomes. Evidence against a prolonged supply shock would include stable cargo delivery, verified passage and lower physical-market price spreads.

**External AI boom with concentrated Chinese and American risks.** Continued computing capital expenditure can support exports while Chinese internal demand lags and US financing costs remain elevated. Declining equipment orders, financing cancellations or expanded trade restrictions would challenge the case.

**Broader demand recovery.** Favorable CPI, better credit conditions and successive increases in real orders might eventually turn the equity rebound into sustained activity. One day's stocks, an expectation survey and a nominal export figure are not enough.

The near-term evidence calendar is specific: **US CPI and Copom minutes on 10 November, Brazil's October IPCA on 12 November, and US October PPI on 13 November**. These releases are not incorporated as outcomes in this early Tuesday edition. [8][12][13]
