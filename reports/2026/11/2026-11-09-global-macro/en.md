# Global Macro — Asia rebounds into the US inflation test, but the return of risk appetite has limits

**9 November 2026 · Early Monday edition, Brasília time · Information cutoff: approximately 00:54 BRT (03:54 UTC).** Asian Monday trading had begun; most European and American cash-equity markets had not yet opened their Monday sessions. The latest completed US and Brazilian cash close is **Friday, 6 November**. October US consumer inflation is scheduled for **Tuesday, 10 November** and must not be treated as released. Differences in session, country, time zone and measurement basis are explicit. [1][2][3]

Asian shares recovered part of the previous week's losses as immediate concerns about AI valuations and Middle Eastern escalation eased. Reuters reported the MSCI Asia-Pacific index excluding Japan up approximately **0.8%**, the Nikkei **2.5%** and Taiwanese equities **2.5%** in early Monday trade. Brent was around **US$97.49 a barrel** at that point, below Friday's **US$98.32** futures close. The dollar was firm after US October payroll employment rose **165,000**, strengthening market expectations of a Federal Reserve rate increase in December. These numbers describe **Monday indications and Friday settlements**, not synchronized closing prices. [1][2][5][6]

## 2. Why Friday's US employment release still governs Monday trading

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics reported **165,000** additional nonfarm jobs for October, versus average monthly payroll gains of **48,000** over the preceding twelve months. August and September payrolls were revised up a combined **84,000**. The household unemployment rate declined to **4.1%**, while the number working part-time for economic reasons rose to **4.8 million**, up **339,000**. These results jointly point to continued hiring alongside labor-underutilization weaknesses. [2]

Sector concentration matters. Professional and business services contributed **70,000** jobs and healthcare **42,000**. The combined strength of those categories does not establish a universal pickup in factory utilization, housing construction or consumer discretionary spending. Likewise, involuntary part-time work and long-duration unemployment cannot be erased from the assessment merely because the U-3 unemployment rate declined. The establishment and household surveys employ different denominators, revisions and reference populations.

Average hourly earnings increased **0.4%** in October and **3.1%** over the year in nominal terms. Wages support spending, but their real purchasing-power implications depend on inflation over comparable periods. The October CPI to be released Tuesday therefore matters more than extrapolating nominal compensation alone. A payroll report can lower the perceived near-term risk of recession while raising the expected duration of monetary restriction when inflation is still high. [2][3]

## 3. The CPI decision boundary: three outcomes, not a forecast disguised as fact

The Bureau of Labor Statistics schedule places the October US CPI release on **Tuesday, 10 November**, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern time. At the editorial cutoff there is no official October value to insert into a trading or inflation table. Analysts' estimates vary and are forecasts, not realized measurements. This creates an asymmetric information situation: Friday payrolls are observed while the matching October price reading is not. [3]

A **higher and broad-based core and headline inflation reading** would make it harder to argue that the energy shock was transitory and fully offset by weakening demand. Expected short rates might rise; longer rates could behave differently if inflation fears impair long-term purchasing power or raise recession risk. A **lower reading concentrated in fuel prices** would provide partial relief, but would not automatically prove that rents, service costs and wages have normalized. A **broad decline in underlying inflation** would be stronger evidence for disinflation than one volatile energy component.

The relevance extends beyond US policy. Global dollar yields help price emerging-market external debt, bank wholesale funding and dollar-denominated commodity inventories. Yet the movement of a borrowing cost is not an observed cross-border fund flow or a change in legal asset ownership. Brazilian companies with foreign-currency liabilities, Asian exporters and European banks may face different hedging and maturity structures. [3][4]

```flow
Observed October US employment resilience → Lower immediate hard-landing fears → More restrictive expected future US policy
Tuesday CPI above expectation → Increased inflation persistence concern → Potential pressure on short yields, dollar funding and risky assets
Tuesday CPI below expectation → Reduced near-term inflation concern → Potential relief to duration valuations, conditional on broader components
Reported Gulf maritime insecurity → Freight and insurance risk → Delivered energy costs and importers' purchasing power
```

Each arrow is conditional, not a causal estimate from one session. Yield reactions may be dominated by risk aversion or term premia; exchange-rate changes can hedge or amplify transmission. The economic interpretation must be revised after observing actual CPI components and policy communication.

## 4. The AI correction and the partial Asian rebound

Global technology markets experienced a sharp correction during the previous week. On Friday the US S&P 500 gained **0.31%** and Nasdaq **1.09%**, but ended the week **2.7%** and **3.4%** lower, respectively. Nikkei and South Korean stocks had suffered much larger Friday declines before the new Asian Monday rebound. The contrast between a negative weekly outcome and a positive early Monday opening is ordinary financial volatility, not proof that questions about AI infrastructure profitability were settled over the weekend. [1][5]

A stock's price represents discounted expectations of profits, risks and financing conditions. A sell-off can occur without a corresponding cancellation of data-center construction or chip production. Meanwhile, actual infrastructure projects have capex budgets, financing commitments, order schedules and electricity procurement. Changes in those variables usually arrive with longer lags than daily returns. The correct industrial tests are newly announced and revised company capital expenditure, verified cancellations, chip equipment backlog and power-grid connection spending.

Asia has different positions in the technology chain. Taiwanese chip manufacturing, Japanese capital equipment, Korean memory and broader Chinese industrial production are not interchangeable. AI-related sales can be resilient even when consumer electronics is weak, and international export orders can decline even while specialized chip orders expand. China's official October manufacturing PMI at **49.3**, with export orders at **48.7**, describes the breadth of factory conditions rather than the output of an individual foundry. [9]

One explanation for Monday's recovery is that investors judged the previous week's pricing to have overreacted; another is geopolitical relief following signals of continuing US-Iran talks. Both interpretations remain hypotheses. Confirmatory evidence would be sustained normal risk financing, stable earnings revisions and actual supply-chain orders. A single intraday bounce cannot establish any of those outcomes. [1]

## 5. Energy: expected de-escalation and transport danger coexist

Reuters reported early Monday Brent near **US$97.49**, down from Friday's **US$98.32** settlement, as comments about the prospect of avoiding new attacks on Iran eased some perceived risk. Crude still trades in the context of the broader US-Iran conflict and concerns about maritime safety. The last completed week had involved a pronounced fall in oil futures prices despite reported incidents affecting vessels. The next physical test is not the headline commodity quote alone but the cost and continuity of shipping to importers. [1][6]

Oil-market exposure begins with the physical grade and supply point. Refiners face different crude characteristics and contractual pricing; shipping companies face route diversion, charter duration and insurance charges; importing households face diesel, gasoline, electricity and transport costs with lags. A futures decline can arrive before contracts or delivered cargoes reflect relief. Conversely, an isolated incident can raise insurance quotes without establishing a statistically measurable global loss of delivered barrels.

The relation between oil and monetary policy is also conditional. If delivered energy costs fall sustainably, headline inflation pressure may ease; but service inflation can persist. If costs rise while manufacturing weakens, monetary authorities face the familiar supply-shock dilemma. Production exporters and import-dependent economies will not share the same fiscal or purchasing-power effects. The actual distribution depends on volumes, tax systems, subsidy rules and hedging.

## 6. Brazil: external rate pressure meets local credit and fiscal constraints

Brazil's last completed equity session, Friday 6 November, ended with the Ibovespa at **168,498.45 points**, up **1.39%** that day. The dollar was quoted around **R$5.50**. The Copom had held Selic at **13.75%** on 4 November. Monday domestic stock and currency outcomes were not yet available at the cutoff. A higher expected US policy rate can change required returns on Brazilian assets, but it does not retroactively explain the Brazilian session before the US CPI release. [10]

Three domestic channels determine how the external picture reaches households and firms. Dollar appreciation can increase the local-currency cost of imported machinery and energy while increasing translated revenue for exporters. A restrictive Selic raises working-capital and household credit costs, but also supports returns on domestic fixed-income assets. Fiscal expectations influence term premia and foreign investor confidence through anticipated revenue, spending, refinancing and institutional credibility. These channels sometimes offset one another.

Brazil's position as an agricultural, mineral and energy exporter does not guarantee uniform benefit from higher commodity prices. A producer may gain margin while transporters and consumers lose purchasing power. Budget revenue effects depend on output, contracts, taxes and royalty design. A stronger Ibovespa can coexist with currency depreciation and weaker credit availability outside large listed firms.

The next local empirical test requires IPCA outcomes, Focus expectations, local yield curves and official fiscal execution, not only a view about Wall Street. A financing and ownership analysis also needs actual capital flows and balances rather than inferring money entering Brazil from a daily equity-index gain. The CPI release in the US can affect risk pricing internationally without determining all Brazilian inflation dynamics.

## 7. Europe and China: two sources of global divergence

The Bank of England raised Bank Rate to **4.00%** on 5 November amid concerns that the energy shock would keep UK inflation high. Eurostat's preliminary October HICP rate for the euro area was **4.0%** year on year; the energy component was **20.8%** and the core excluding food, energy, alcohol and tobacco **2.5%**. These refer to different jurisdictions and statistical objects. A change in the British policy rate does not mean the European Central Bank made the same decision. [7][8]

Euro-area inflation pressure makes the recent easing of crude-oil futures potentially important, yet the transmission to consumers depends on electricity market design, import contracts and taxation. European industrial activity can remain weak even as reduced delivered energy costs improve margins. Highly leveraged governments and businesses are also exposed to long-duration financing prices. A European fund inflow is not evidence that the region has escaped those constraints.

The latest Chinese official manufacturing PMI, **49.3** for October, signals broad factory softness in the surveyed population. The reading for new export orders, **48.7**, is a diffusion index, not a 48.7% annual fall in exports. The distinction matters as the US tariff environment, global equipment orders and Asian AI spending evolve separately. No direct comparison of China's PMI points and US payroll headcounts can establish whose economy is growing faster. [9]

Trade, credit and energy connect these regions: weak export orders may reduce demand for industrial raw materials, while expensive energy affects European and Asian importers differently. Monetary reactions depend on domestic price expectations and labor markets. The idea of a single global interest-rate stance therefore obscures material national heterogeneity.

## 8. Scenarios and observable falsifiers

| Competing mechanism | Evidence that would support it | Evidence against it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employment remains resilient but inflation prolongs US high rates | Higher broad-based CPI, sticky core components, short yields and dollar credit costs | Broad disinflation, weak job hours, declining inflation expectations |
| AI financial correction fades without industrial contraction | Stabilizing earnings, capital expenditure and actual equipment orders | Canceled builds, lower supplier backlogs and wider financing spreads |
| Oil de-escalation reduces energy-driven inflation | Sustained safe ship passage, declining freight and landed fuel costs | Renewed verified disruptions, higher insurance and delivered prices |
| Fragmented growth worsens cross-border credit | Weaker Chinese orders, wider spreads and financing failures in exposed borrowers | Recovering industrial orders and stable issuance and trade credit |

No scenario probability is inferred from a handful of stock prices. Financial and real-economy effects may diverge for weeks. Correlation between a Brent move, an index rebound and a political statement is not proof of exclusive causation. Better evidence consists of market instrument detail, physical delivery observations, official prices and audited investment expenditure.

## 9. What the opening of the week can and cannot establish

Monday brings additional US Treasury issuance and company announcements; Tuesday's October CPI is a particularly consequential data release. Later in the week, initial unemployment claims and October producer prices can help distinguish employment resilience from persistent cost increases. The announced dates are not observed results, and the ultimate Fed decision for December remains open. [3][11]

The most important balance-sheet interaction is between expected US policy rates, global credit spreads and short-term liquidity demand. Large subscriptions to money-market funds can reflect defensive allocation without proving equity liquidation. Exporters and importers of oil can face different cash-flow changes, and technology companies can suffer share-price pressure without yet changing real capital expenditure. Any claim that one channel dominates should be reassessed as the new official data arrive.

At the cutoff the observable regime is **a selective Asian risk-asset rebound, continued policy-rate uncertainty following strong US hiring, an upcoming US inflation test, and still-contested physical energy security**. Friday's completed market closes and Monday's partial Asian indications must not be combined into a fictitious synchronized global session.
