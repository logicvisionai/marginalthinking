# Global Monetary Institutions — policy paths diverge while balance-sheet channels tighten transmission

The monetary system on 7 October is not moving through a synchronized rate cycle. The Federal Reserve, European Central Bank and Bank of Japan have all tightened policy since the previous institutional monitor, the Bank of England has held rates while a third of its committee preferred another increase, and Brazil has moved in the opposite direction with a 25-basis-point Selic cut. The common constraint is energy-driven inflation risk, but the transmission mechanism differs by institution.

The policy-rate comparison alone is incomplete. Balance-sheet and liquidity decisions are now doing more of the work. The Federal Reserve is maintaining an ample-reserves framework while allowing Treasury-bill purchases when needed to preserve reserve abundance. The Bank of England has paired a 3.75% Bank Rate with a multi-year plan to eliminate its remaining monetary-policy gilt portfolio. The Eurosystem continues to let APP and PEPP holdings decline without reinvestment. The Bank of Japan raised its overnight-rate guideline while continuing a pre-announced reduction in government-bond purchases. These choices change sovereign-market duration, bank liquidity and the price at which private balance sheets can refinance.

Brazil is the clearest counterexample to a global-tightening narrative. The Copom cut the Selic target to 13.75% on 16 September, continuing a gradual easing cycle while still describing expectations as above target and the external environment as uncertain. The result is a global configuration in which policy direction is increasingly jurisdiction-specific even when the underlying shock — energy, inflation persistence and expensive long-duration finance — is shared.

## The Fed has tightened the rate while preserving an ample-reserves regime

The FOMC raised the federal-funds target range to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September by a unanimous 12–0 vote. Its implementation note raised interest on reserve balances to 3.90%, set the standing overnight repo rate at 4.00% and the overnight reverse-repo offering rate at 3.75%, and retained authority to purchase Treasury bills — and, if needed, Treasury securities with three years or less remaining maturity — to maintain ample reserves.

This combination matters because the Fed is tightening the policy price without deliberately engineering reserve scarcity. The operating framework is designed to keep control of the overnight rate even while Treasury supply and long-term yields create separate pressure on financial conditions.

The minutes due later on 7 October will therefore be useful primarily for the committee's reaction function: how participants weighed inflation persistence, demand resilience and financial conditions when they agreed on the September increase. They cannot be treated as a contemporaneous October decision because oil, labour-market data and long yields have moved since the meeting.

## The ECB is tightening both rates and portfolio support

The ECB raised all three key rates by 25 basis points on 10 September. The deposit facility is now 2.50%, the main refinancing rate 2.65% and the marginal lending rate 2.90%. Its September projections put headline inflation at 3.0% in 2026, 2.5% in 2027 and 2.1% in 2028.

At the same time, APP and PEPP portfolios continue to decline because principal payments are no longer reinvested. The policy stance therefore combines a higher short rate with a gradual withdrawal of central-bank balance-sheet support.

That interaction becomes more important when sovereign spreads widen. A single euro-area policy rate transmits through national sovereign curves with different fiscal, liquidity and political risk premia. The ECB's Transmission Protection Instrument remains available for disorderly dynamics, but its existence does not remove ordinary differentiation among sovereign borrowers.

## The Bank of England is holding the rate while accelerating balance-sheet normalization

The Bank of England kept Bank Rate at 3.75% in September by a 6–3 vote. The minority preferred a 25-basis-point increase to 4.00%. The Bank explicitly linked the upside inflation risk to the persistence of the Middle East energy shock; UK CPI inflation was 3.1% in August and was expected to rise further.

The balance-sheet decision was equally important. The MPC unanimously adopted a plan to reduce the remaining stock of government bonds held for monetary-policy purposes to zero, with an average annual unwind of £46 billion through end-2034, including £20 billion of annual sales alongside maturities.

That creates a two-channel stance: the short rate is unchanged, but duration is still being returned to private investors. A "hold" in Bank Rate is therefore not equivalent to no tightening in the broader financial system.

## Japan is normalizing from a different starting point

The Bank of Japan changed its money-market guideline on 18 September, lifting the overnight call-rate target to around 1.25%. The Summary of Opinions published on 1 October shows the underlying debate: some members argued that current activity and prices had not accelerated enough to justify a hike, while the broader normalization logic emphasized underlying inflation moving closer to 2% and the need to adjust monetary accommodation as conditions evolve.

Japan's transmission differs from the Fed, ECB and BoE because the starting nominal rate is much lower and the central bank remains a dominant actor in the JGB market. The September rate increase therefore has to be read together with the schedule for reducing JGB purchases, domestic long yields and the yen.

## Brazil is easing, but not declaring victory over inflation

The Copom reduced the Selic target from 14.00% to 13.75% on 16 September. Its minutes described domestic activity as gradually moderating while remaining resilient and the labour market as still strong. Focus expectations cited by the committee were 4.9% for 2026 and 4.3% for 2027, both above the inflation target.

The easing cycle therefore remains conditional. Brazil can cut while advanced economies tighten because its policy rate started from a much higher level and domestic disinflation has progressed. But imported inflation, exchange-rate repricing and changes in global duration can still constrain the pace of easing.

```flow
Energy shock -> headline inflation and expectations -> central-bank reaction function -> policy-rate decision
Policy rate + balance-sheet policy -> money-market liquidity and sovereign duration -> bank funding and market yields
Funding costs + exchange rates -> household, corporate and government financing -> demand and inflation persistence
Different domestic starting points -> different policy directions even under a shared global shock
```

## What the divergence changes

The first consequence is that exchange-rate transmission becomes more important. When central banks move in different directions, relative expected returns shift even if every institution is responding rationally to its own inflation and activity data.

The second is that "policy rate" becomes a weaker summary of the stance. Asset holdings, reinvestment, reserve supply, standing facilities and government-bond purchases change the amount and duration of risk held by private balance sheets.

The third is fiscal. Higher sovereign yields interact with debt maturity and issuance differently across countries. Monetary-policy divergence can therefore redistribute pressure through currencies, bank balance sheets and government financing even without a synchronized recession.

## What would change this reading

The divergence thesis would weaken if the next round of decisions converges quickly toward a common direction — for example, if energy prices fall persistently enough for the Fed, ECB and BoE to shift toward easing while Brazil slows its cuts.

The balance-sheet channel would become less important if sovereign-market term premia fall materially despite continued portfolio runoff and issuance. Conversely, disorderly sovereign-market moves would make implementation tools and liquidity facilities more important than the headline policy rate.

In Japan, a durable fall in underlying inflation would weaken the case for continued normalization. In Brazil, renewed inflation expectations or exchange-rate pressure would reduce room for additional easing.

## What to watch

The immediate sequence is the FOMC minutes later on 7 October, the evolution of long sovereign yields, energy prices, the ECB's transmission signals, UK wage and inflation data, the Bank of Japan's October meeting, and Brazil's next Focus and Copom communications.

The central institutional question is no longer whether "global central banks" are tightening or easing. It is how different monetary frameworks distribute the same global shocks through rates, balance sheets, liquidity, currencies and sovereign financing.
