# Global Capital Allocation

**Scope:** how global savings, credit, sovereign capital and productive investment are allocated across financial markets, countries and strategic sectors  
**Canonical slug:** `global-capital-allocation`  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-09-16  
**Evidence note:** internal orientation only; public claims require external re-verification.

## Structural assessment

Global capital allocation currently shows dual concentration. Liquid financial wealth remains heavily intermediated through U.S. equity, bond and dollar funding markets, while incremental productive investment is increasingly directed toward strategic physical systems such as AI infrastructure, semiconductors, electricity, grids, energy and critical-mineral processing.

This briefing must always separate stock, flow, valuation and change of control. Growth in market capitalization or external positions is not automatically a capital flow, and bank claims cannot be added to portfolio positions as independent net wealth.

## Economic structure

The system has several distinct channels:

```text
savings → securities markets
bank balance sheets → cross-border credit
corporate capital → FDI / greenfield / M&A
resource and fiscal surpluses → sovereign wealth funds
public policy → strategic infrastructure and industrial finance
```

Each channel has different liquidity, duration, ownership and transmission characteristics.

## Relationships

- U.S. market depth attracts global savings and transmits U.S. rates and valuations globally;
- sovereign wealth funds convert current resource or fiscal income into diversified future claims;
- strategic-sector FDI links capital allocation to power, grid capacity, chips, mineral processing and industrial policy;
- cross-border bank credit can support investment while also accelerating transmission of funding stress;
- high energy or commodity prices can redistribute current income without changing asset ownership.

## Dependencies and constraints

- financial-center concentration creates both liquidity advantages and common exposure to U.S. financial conditions;
- announced greenfield value is not equivalent to realized capex;
- custodial centers complicate geographic attribution of securities flows;
- valuation and FX effects can dominate short-horizon changes in asset stocks;
- strategic investment can be limited by physical infrastructure even when financing is available.

## Structural changes in progress

The durable shift is not a wholesale relocation of the existing financial stock. It is a change in the composition of marginal investment toward infrastructure with high physical, technological and regulatory barriers. This increases the value of electricity, grid access, processing capacity, compute infrastructure and qualified industrial supply chains.

## Open questions

- How much strategic-sector announced investment converts into operating capacity?
- Which jurisdictions can combine financing with power, permitting, equipment and skilled labor quickly enough to absorb new capital?
- Does sovereign capital increasingly substitute for private risk capital in strategic sectors?
- Are higher funding costs changing the geography of infrastructure and industrial investment?
