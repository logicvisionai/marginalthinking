# Lithuania — structural context

Internal research memory. Not a source; reverify current facts externally before reuse.

## Durable mechanisms
- Lithuania's post-1990 model is deliberate institutional embedding: EU, NATO, Schengen and euro membership reinforce sovereignty, market access, security and monetary credibility for a small open economy.
- Historical experience of occupation shapes low tolerance for Russian security risk; this is a durable institutional preference, not merely a current-event reaction.
- Geography magnifies dependence: the Polish land connection, Klaipėda, Baltic interconnectors and proximity to Belarus/Kaliningrad make logistics, energy and defence infrastructure dual-use economic assets.
- Energy decoupling was achieved through infrastructure rather than autarky. LNG, electricity synchronisation, renewables, storage and interconnection reduce Russian-system dependence while preserving regional dependence.
- Defence spending is now large enough to alter fiscal composition and labour/capital allocation. The German brigade and related infrastructure can create productive capital or crowd out civilian investment.
- Convergence remains substantial but easy catch-up gains are fading. Labour scarcity, ageing and regional demographic decline increasingly bind productivity.
- Manufacturing, photonics/lasers and fintech show how small scale can coexist with global niches when EU market/regulatory access complements specialised human capital.

## Research questions to carry forward
- Does security investment raise durable infrastructure and industrial capacity or mainly absorb scarce labour and fiscal space?
- Can migration and productivity offset ageing enough to sustain convergence?
- Do renewables, storage and interconnection convert energy security into export/industrial advantage?
- How does Poland's connector role affect Lithuanian logistics and security resilience?
