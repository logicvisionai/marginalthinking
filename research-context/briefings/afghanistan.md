# Afghanistan — post-2021 state capacity and regional constraints

**Object:** Afghanistan as a durable political-economy and regional-security research context.  
**Role:** Internal orientation only; not a public source. Re-verify all current facts externally before reuse.

## Structural assessment

Afghanistan's post-2021 order is best understood as a recomposition of state capacity rather than a simple collapse/recovery sequence. The internationally backed republic combined domestic institutions with an unusually large external fiscal, aid and security architecture. After the August 2021 rupture, the de facto authorities consolidated nationwide territorial and administrative control and strengthened domestic revenue collection, but operate with a much smaller public-resource envelope, restricted international financial connectivity, limited recognition and weaker capacity to finance broad services and investment.

This creates an important analytical separation: territorial control and revenue extraction can strengthen while per-capita welfare, financial intermediation, public-service capacity and human-capital formation remain weak. Aggregate output recovery is therefore not equivalent to development or household recovery.

## Historical anchors

- Afghan state formation has repeatedly involved a difficult center-periphery bargain shaped by mountainous geography, control of routes and customs points, coercive capacity, local authority and external finance. This is more useful than treating decentralization or resistance to central authority as an immutable national characteristic.
- The late-nineteenth-century Durand Line became the inherited Afghanistan-Pakistan boundary and embedded cross-border communities, trade, refugee movement and security into a persistent bilateral constraint.
- Soviet intervention, the post-1989 withdrawal of support, the 1990s civil war and the first Taliban emirate established durable patterns of external patronage, armed networks and fragmented authority.
- The 2001–2021 republic expanded schooling, health, infrastructure, communications and urban services, but grants financed a very large share of total public expenditure and security spending. The 2021 transition therefore removed not only a government but much of the fiscal-security architecture supporting aggregate demand and public capacity.

## Political and institutional structure

The de facto authorities have centralized territorial control and revenue enforcement more successfully than many immediate post-2021 expectations implied. This does not imply broad political inclusion, international recognition or institutional normalization. Future research should keep separate: country, de facto government, Taliban movement, security institutions, armed opposition, households and population groups.

Revenue mobilization is a meaningful state-capacity signal, but a higher revenue-to-GDP ratio does not establish adequate or transparent expenditure. Customs and border control matter disproportionately because trade taxation links fiscal capacity directly to corridor politics.

Restrictions on women and girls are structurally relevant beyond rights analysis: they constrain the future supply of teachers, health workers and skilled labour, reduce household earning opportunities, complicate aid delivery and weaken human-capital accumulation. Re-verify the current legal and implementation status before reuse.

## Economy and production

The economy has shifted from an aid-supported demand structure toward a smaller domestic fiscal base, informal/private activity and regional trade. The durable constraint is that stronger collection does not by itself replace lost grants, deep banking connectivity, investment finance or public capital formation.

Agriculture, construction, mining and services can support aggregate recovery, but rapid population growth and return migration can cause per-capita outcomes to deteriorate even while headline GDP rises. Afghanistan's statistical base is limited enough that estimates from major institutions can differ materially by reference period and methodology; preserve those distinctions rather than synthesizing them into false precision.

Mineral endowment should not be treated as investable productive capacity. Commercial development depends on infrastructure, power, transport, finance, legal certainty, processing capability, security and external market access.

## Society, labour and demography

Large return migration from Pakistan and Iran increases labour supply and consumption needs faster than capital formation can necessarily absorb them. The mechanism to track is not migration alone but the interaction among jobs, housing, food, public services, remittances, local infrastructure and household debt/coping strategies.

Human-capital erosion is a long-horizon constraint. Education restrictions, weak household incomes and reduced service capacity can lower future labour productivity even if short-run fiscal and security indicators appear stable.

## Geography, relationships and dependencies

Afghanistan is landlocked between Iran, Central Asia, Pakistan and a narrow connection to China. Mountain systems fragment domestic markets and make border corridors economically consequential.

Pakistan has historically provided important access to seaports and markets, so border closure or political friction transmits into imports, food, exports, customs revenue and humanitarian logistics. Western routes through Iran and northern routes through Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan provide diversification rather than full substitution.

Regional electricity and infrastructure links can reduce isolation incrementally. CASA-1000 and northern power/fuel links are examples of projects whose significance lies in corridor integration and energy access; current construction/operational status must be re-verified.

Russia, China, Iran, India, Pakistan, the Gulf states and Central Asian governments engage Afghanistan through different combinations of diplomacy, trade, security and infrastructure. Recognition status and bilateral arrangements are time-sensitive and must be checked anew.

## Security structure

The security environment changed after 2021 rather than becoming simply "stable." Nationwide conventional warfare declined relative to the pre-2021 period, while ISIL-K, localized armed opposition, unexploded ordnance, repression/rights violations and Afghanistan-Pakistan hostilities remain distinct risk classes. Pakistan's claims regarding armed groups operating from Afghan territory and denials by the de facto authorities must remain attributed unless independently verified.

Border security and trade are tightly coupled: deterioration in Afghanistan-Pakistan relations can affect both violence and economic corridors simultaneously.

## Open questions

- Can domestic revenue and regional trade expand fast enough to finance durable public capacity without the former scale of external grants?
- Which revenue categories are most sustainable, and how much depends on import/customs flows?
- Can alternative corridors through Iran and Central Asia materially reduce dependence on Pakistan without creating new concentrated dependencies?
- How quickly are education and labour restrictions translating into measurable shortages in health, education and skilled occupations?
- Can mining move from geological potential to commercially financed production under current infrastructure, legal and financial constraints?
- How do mass returns alter urbanisation, labour absorption, food security and local political pressures over several years?
- Which Afghanistan-Pakistan security mechanisms most directly transmit into trade and humanitarian access?
