# Portugal — structural context

Internal research memory. Not a source; reverify current facts externally before reuse.

## Durable mechanisms
- Portugal's modern political economy is anchored in democratisation, EU membership and euro-area institutions. EU integration expanded infrastructure, trade access and regulatory capacity while euro membership shifted adjustment away from exchange-rate depreciation toward productivity, wages, margins and reallocation.
- Post-sovereign-crisis fiscal repair reduced debt vulnerability and strengthened banking stability, but lower debt changes rather than removes the fiscal constraint because ageing and investment compete for resources.
- Productivity, firm scale and weak diffusion remain the core convergence problem. Employment and migration can lift aggregate output, but sustained income convergence requires output per worker/hour and firm scaling.
- Tourism is an external-balance engine but concentrates housing, infrastructure and labour pressures in high-productivity regions.
- Immigration reversed population decline for a period without eliminating ageing; housing supply and skilled-worker retention connect demographic policy directly to productivity.
- Renewable abundance, Iberian integration, Sines logistics/digital infrastructure and Atlantic geography can support new productive capacity, but grid resilience and limited continental interconnection constrain the upside.
- The Recovery and Resilience Plan is a temporary investment discontinuity. The structural test is whether projects become persistent productivity gains rather than a post-programme investment cliff.

## Research questions to carry forward
- Does post-recovery-fund investment remain high enough to sustain productivity convergence?
- Can housing supply expand in productive regions without displacing labour and worsening youth emigration?
- Can renewable electricity and Sines become industrial advantages rather than mainly infrastructure assets?
- How does ageing alter the fiscal trade-off between social spending, defence and productive investment?
