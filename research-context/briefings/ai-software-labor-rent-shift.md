# AI, software scarcity and labour-market formation

**Scope:** the migration of economic scarcity as AI lowers the cost of cognitive tasks and generic software while infrastructure, distribution, institutional trust and skilled judgment remain constrained  
**Canonical slug:** `ai-software-labor-rent-shift`  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-07  
**Evidence note:** internal orientation only; all current claims require external re-verification.

## Structural assessment

Cheaper generation of code, text, analysis and routine decisions can increase competition in application features without eliminating the demand for software. The economically relevant distinction is between *producing a feature* and *owning a durable bottleneck*: proprietary data, workflow integration, switching costs, distribution, assurance, computing capacity, electricity and capital. Application-level abundance can coexist with concentrated infrastructure and distribution rents. Neither a broad collapse of SaaS nor economy-wide unemployment follows automatically.

## Economic and production structure

- Software value can migrate from generic features and seat licences toward measured actions, completed outcomes, trusted integrations, data access and compliance. Seat-based pricing may persist when users still receive distinct value; changing pricing models is an empirical question, not a deterministic endpoint.
- Infrastructure scaling is physical: GPUs, high-bandwidth memory, data centres, grid connections, cooling, equipment, permits and finance can bind even as inference efficiency improves. Separate model cost per task, total task demand and aggregate electricity consumption.
- Valuation compression in established SaaS and rising AI investment are different indicators; neither proves the other caused it. Distinguish observed multiples, revenue growth, margins, realized returns and announced capital expenditure.
- Cloud concentration and interoperability affect who captures rents. Model portability alone does not remove data-egress, workflow, identity, procurement and regulatory switching costs.

## Society, labour and institutions

- Study **tasks and hiring cohorts** before predicting occupations' disappearance. A firm may retain experienced staff while reducing junior intake, even without large layoffs. Entry-level work also trains future experts; removing routine tasks can weaken apprenticeship and institutional knowledge unless training is redesigned.
- AI can complement less-experienced workers and accelerate learning in some measured workflows. Exposure is not displacement, and task productivity is not a universal organization-wide productivity gain. Seek contrary evidence and independent field studies.
- Occupational exposure differs across education, gender, income groups and countries because task composition differs. The location where AI is used need not be the location where cloud, compute or intellectual-property income accrues.
- Political and institutional choices condition distribution: competition enforcement, interoperability, privacy, labour training, procurement, grid cost allocation and the right to contest automated decisions. Technical capability alone does not determine bargaining power, working time or autonomy.

## Relationships and constraints

```text
cheaper model-enabled tasks → cheaper generic features → application competition / pricing redesign
AI deployment at scale → chips + memory + data centres + electricity + distribution → possible bottleneck rents
automation of junior tasks → fewer supervised repetitions (possible) → future specialist formation constraint
institutional evaluation + permissions + human escalation → deployable workflow reliability → adoption and accountability
```

## Open questions

- Does hiring of exposed younger cohorts recover as adoption expands, and do firms preserve apprenticeship?
- Where do outcome/action pricing models actually improve customer value and provider margins?
- Can interoperability and lower inference costs reduce infrastructure concentration, or do data, distribution and grid constraints remain binding?
- Which productivity gains persist in independent production settings, and who receives the resulting income or free time?
- How do energy tariffs, permitting and local opposition redistribute the cost of data-centre growth?
