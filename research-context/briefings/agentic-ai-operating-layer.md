# Agentic AI operating layer

## Structural assessment
Enterprise AI is moving from isolated assistance toward delegated execution across tools and organizational context. The durable constraint is increasingly the control system around the model: identity, least-privilege permissions, context segmentation, workflow-specific evaluation, auditability, recovery and economical human escalation. Model capability remains necessary but is not sufficient for reliable institutional deployment.

## Institutional and production structure
The economically relevant unit is increasingly an evaluated workflow rather than a standalone model. Organizations can preserve part of their operating capability across model changes when they retain permission systems, context boundaries, tool interfaces, regression tests, logs and recovery procedures. This shifts part of competitive advantage toward workflow engineering and assurance infrastructure.

Selective autonomy is an important design pattern: attempt the task, measure decision-time reliability, allow a sufficiently reliable subset to proceed, and defer uncertain or high-impact cases to humans. This can make human review a scarce resource allocated by risk rather than a universal step, but only if the reliability signal itself remains calibrated in production.

## Society, labour and accountability
Near-term labour transmission should be framed through task composition before aggregate employment claims. Longer agent workflows can move human work toward specification, review, exception handling and workflow design, while the distribution of gains depends on ownership of workflows, redesign capacity and how productivity gains are shared. Agentic systems also expand governance from content control to action control because tool-using systems can alter records, code, communications and other external states.

## Durable constraints
- Context integrity and privacy can deteriorate when agents receive broad institutional information.
- Multi-task and interdependent work can expose reliability failures hidden by isolated benchmarks.
- Human supervision can erase economic gains if escalation requirements grow nearly one-for-one with agent activity.
- Continuous evaluation is structurally important because software interfaces, policies, permissions and retrieved data change after deployment.
- Audit, rollback and recovery become production infrastructure when agents can take consequential actions.

## Open questions
- Do independent production studies confirm durable productivity gains rather than usage growth?
- Can contextual-integrity and multi-task failure rates fall materially under realistic deployment?
- Does selective autonomy reduce human workload without increasing residual error?
- Which sectors standardize agent identity, permission and continuous-evaluation infrastructure first?
- How are productivity gains distributed between firms, workers and infrastructure providers?
