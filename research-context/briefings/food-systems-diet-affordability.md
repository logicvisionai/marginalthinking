# Food Systems and Diet Affordability

**Scope:** connections between food production, household purchasing power, distribution and nutrition.
**Canonical slug:** `food-systems-diet-affordability`
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-12
**Evidence note:** internal orientation only; reverify public claims externally.

## Structural assessment

Agricultural output and household access to nutritious food are different outcomes. The chain is production, storage, transport, retail, relative food prices, household disposable income, diet quality and longer-term health. A good harvest does not establish adequate purchasing power or dietary diversity. Roads, refrigeration, sanitation and market structure can matter more than farm-gate prices for perishable foods.

## Institutions and distribution

Agriculture, health, transport, education and social protection agencies govern different parts of the chain. Low-income households and net food buyers may lose from higher prices even when some producers gain. Nutritional outcomes depend on income, sanitation, education and healthcare as well as food availability. FAO food-security and affordability indicators and WHO food-safety indicators have different definitions and cannot be added together.

## Dependencies and questions

Import dependence creates currency and shipping exposure. Agricultural exporters may still have domestic affordability gaps. Distinguish food-group prices from general food inflation, and measured illness from expanded surveillance. Test whether logistics savings reach consumers and whether targeted support improves diet diversity.
