# Cross-Border Price Transmission

**Scope:** transmission from border prices to production costs, domestic inflation, terms of trade and real incomes across trading partners and monetary areas.  
**Canonical slug:** `cross-border-price-transmission`  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-16  
**Evidence note:** internal research orientation only; all current figures and institutional claims require external re-verification.

## Structural assessment

Import/export price indexes, producer-price indexes and consumer-price indexes describe different baskets, weights, currencies and transaction stages. An upstream price change does not establish a one-to-one downstream pass-through coefficient. Contracts, invoice currency, inventories, freight, tariffs, margins, demand and delivery lags determine where costs are absorbed or passed on.

Bilateral terms-of-trade indexes measure export purchasing power relative to imports for a particular trading-partner basket. The difference between aggregate monthly export- and import-price changes is **not** a measured bilateral or economy-wide terms-of-trade gain; nor does a price index establish real trade volume, the trade balance, corporate profit or beneficial ownership of the resulting income.

## Political, economic and social transmission

- **Production:** imported fuel, industrial inputs and capital equipment affect different supply chains. Distinguish fuel from nonfuel inputs, and intermediate goods from final consumer goods.
- **Institutions:** a shared monetary policy does not equalize national energy mixes, regulated tariffs, tax systems, contract structures or fiscal capacity. National HICP dispersion is an observed outcome, not proof of any single cause or a ranking of policy competence.
- **Distribution:** essential-energy and imported-input costs can compress household real purchasing power before nominal wages adjust; incidence depends on consumption weights, income, employer exposure and the reach of public relief.
- **External financing:** import-cost increases can coincide with higher export prices yet still strain firms needing imported equipment or foreign-currency debt service. Test volumes, debt denomination, exchange rates and refinancing terms separately.

## Open questions

- Which imported cost increases survive inventories and margin adjustment to appear in final prices, and with what lag?
- Where do bilateral price gains coincide with volume losses or unequal household purchasing-power effects?
- How much national inflation dispersion reflects energy exposure, administered prices, taxes or domestic service inflation?
