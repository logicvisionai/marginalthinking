# Türkiye — structural context

Internal research memory. Not a source; reverify current facts externally before reuse.

## Durable mechanisms
- Türkiye is a connector economy across EU manufacturing, the Black Sea, Mediterranean, Caucasus and Middle East. The Straits, customs-union integration and energy corridors make openness of surrounding systems a direct economic variable.
- Republican state capacity and earlier state-led industrialisation created infrastructure, engineering and supplier capabilities later reoriented toward private export manufacturing. Post-1980 liberalisation widened export capacity but also exposure to foreign capital and exchange-rate cycles.
- The EU Customs Union is a structural production institution even while accession remains politically stalled: automotive, machinery, appliances and supplier ecosystems tie Turkish industrial performance to European demand and standards.
- The macro regime combines low sovereign debt and functioning banks with recurring inflation/lira instability and large gross external-financing needs. Durable disinflation matters because it changes savings maturity, cost of capital and corporate currency behaviour, not only the CPI path.
- Energy security is diversification rather than autarky: LNG, renewables and Black Sea gas reduce concentration while import dependence remains material. Electricity is more domestically diversified than gas.
- Defence procurement and exports have built industrial learning, but export value is not equivalent to technological autonomy; imported components, certification and external technology remain constraints.
- NATO membership, the Turkish Straits and simultaneous economic/security ties with Russia and Ukraine create leverage and obligations rather than simple bloc alignment.
- Migration, earthquake reconstruction, regional inequality and the Kurdish question interact with labour supply, fiscal allocation, urban capital and security policy.

## Research questions to carry forward
- Does disinflation become an institutional regime or another temporary stabilisation episode?
- Does industrial scale translate into a higher share of high-technology exports and civilian spillovers from defence capability?
- Do energy diversification and grid investment materially lower external vulnerability?
- How do refugee transition, demographic ageing and female labour participation alter labour scarcity and fiscal capacity?
