# Global Reserve System

**Scope:** official foreign-exchange reserves, reserve-currency composition, official gold and the institutional mechanisms through which sovereign balance sheets manage liquidity, counterparty risk and geopolitical exposure  
**Canonical slug:** `global-reserve-system`  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-09-16  
**Evidence note:** internal orientation only; public claims require external re-verification.

## Structural assessment

The reserve system remains centered on the dollar because reserve-currency power depends on more than currency share: liquidity, collateral, market depth, convertibility, settlement infrastructure and the scale of safe assets reinforce one another. Diversification is real but should be measured as a change in portfolio composition rather than assumed to be a binary replacement process.

Gold occupies a different role from reserve currencies. It is not another state's liability and therefore can reduce counterparty and sanctions exposure at the margin, but it does not provide the same settlement, collateral and market functions as dollar assets.

## Historical anchors

The useful long-run frame is the gradual decline of the dollar share of reported reserves from much higher late-1990s levels into the mid-to-high 50% range by 2025-26, combined with stronger official gold demand since 2022. Methodological revisions to COFER and later revisions to official-sector gold estimates mean apparent shifts must be separated from statistical changes and valuation effects.

## Relationships

- reserve-currency composition interacts with sovereign bond-market depth, cross-border banking, sanctions exposure and trade settlement;
- official gold demand interacts with reserve diversification but should not be interpreted as a one-for-one sale of dollars;
- exchange-rate movements can change reported reserve shares without equivalent portfolio transactions;
- sovereign wealth funds are adjacent but analytically distinct: they convert public surpluses into longer-duration ownership rather than primarily serving reserve-liquidity functions.

## Dependencies and constraints

- no current substitute combines the scale, openness, collateral role and liquidity of the dollar-centered system;
- capital controls and market structure limit the reserve role of some alternatives;
- quarterly currency-share changes can be dominated by valuation;
- official gold estimates are revised and may rely on non-official-sector inference;
- reserve diversification can occur without weakening dollar centrality in funding and collateral markets.

## Structural changes in progress

The durable change is toward a more hybrid sovereign balance sheet: major reserve currencies remain central while gold and smaller currencies gain marginal weight. The pace is uneven and should be tested against transaction data rather than narrative claims of rapid de-dollarization.

## Open questions

- Does diversification broaden gradually across several reserve assets or concentrate mainly in gold?
- Can any alternative asset class replicate the combination of liquidity, collateral and scale provided by U.S. markets?
- How much observed reserve-share change reflects transactions versus valuation and methodology?
- Do sanctions and geopolitical risk materially change reserve-management behavior across a broad set of central banks?
