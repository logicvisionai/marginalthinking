# Livestock Health and One Health Institutions

**Scope:** institutional coordination among veterinary services, food safety, public health and livestock producers.
**Canonical slug:** `livestock-health-one-health-institutions`
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-26
**Evidence note:** internal orientation only; reverify public claims externally.

## Structural assessment

Animal-health governance links farm production, veterinary services, inspection, laboratories and trade. Production volume alone does not measure safety. Preventive care and diagnostic capacity have costs that differ by farm size, geography and access to public services. Policy evaluation must distinguish actions, observed outcomes and changes in reporting coverage.

## Institutional structure

FAO, WHO, WOAH and UNEP coordinate the One Health approach internationally. National agencies retain responsibility for inspection, reporting and implementation. International guidance and announced plans are not evidence that outcomes improved. Changes in recorded cases may reflect expanded laboratory capacity or altered sampling.

## Economy and society

Smaller producers may face greater fixed compliance costs. Sanitary infrastructure, refrigeration and traceability can support consumer protection and export access, but benefits and costs fall on different actors. Food processing workers and consumers face different exposures. The effectiveness of prevention depends on finance, veterinary access, oversight and implementation.

## 2026-11-26 update — AMR implementation and measurement

The 23 November 2026 report `MT-BIO-2026-11-23-amr-implementation-gap` adds a durable implementation-and-measurement baseline to the October livestock-AMR briefing. Use the indicators as separate evidence streams, not as a merged causal series:

- **WHO TrACSS 2026:** 173 of 183 responding countries report an AMR action plan; 22% report both funding and full implementation; 36% report a national reference laboratory with selected testing capacity. TrACSS is a national self-assessment, not an independent expenditure or service-quality audit.
- **WOAH ANIMUSE 2024:** 119 participants enter the global quantity analysis and cover an estimated 74% of food-producing animal biomass. The report gives 83,254 tonnes submitted and up to 86,346 tonnes after coverage adjustment; only five participants use farm records for validated quantitative data. Sales/import records do not establish the quantity administered at farm level.
- **ECDC/WHO Europe 2025 invasive-isolate surveillance:** 21/43 reporting countries have at least 50% third-generation-cephalosporin resistance in `K. pneumoniae`; 21/38 have at least 50% carbapenem resistance in `Acinetobacter`. EU incidence of carbapenem-resistant `K. pneumoniae` bloodstream infection is 3.75 per 100,000 in 2025, 72% above 2019.
- **ESAC-Net 2025:** EU population-weighted consumption is 18.9 defined daily doses per 1,000 inhabitants per day, 5% below 2019; Access antibiotics are 62.5% of EU consumption. The separate 17.3 EU/EEA community-consumption indicator has a different scope and must not be compared directly with EU targets.

These series measure national implementation, intended animal-use quantities, invasive human isolates and human consumption respectively. They do not link matched country/species/pathogen/exposure/time data and do not establish animal-to-human causation. Preserve the distinction between improved reporting coverage and real outcome change.
