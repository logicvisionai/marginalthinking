# Ukraine — structural context

Internal research memory. Not a source; reverify current facts externally before reuse.

## Durable mechanisms
- Ukraine's war economy operates through two linked balance sheets: domestic revenues/borrowing are heavily directed to defence while external grants and concessional finance sustain civilian services, reserves and reconstruction. Partner-financing continuity is therefore part of the macro operating architecture.
- Monetary stability is exceptional rather than normal peacetime equilibrium: FX management, official inflows, reserves and banking continuity jointly contain pressures created by damaged exports, imports, defence and reconstruction.
- Reconstruction is already occurring during war and can change the composition of capital stock, not merely replace it: decentralised energy, logistics, housing, digital administration, defence manufacturing and EU-compatible infrastructure are potential structural shifts.
- EU accession is an operating reform framework linking budget support and reconstruction capital to governance, regulatory and anti-corruption capacity.
- Demographic rupture may be the largest long-run economic loss. Displacement, emigration, mobilisation and skill shortages affect the future tax base, housing geography and private-investment returns.
- Energy, transport and housing are simultaneously civilian-capacity, resilience and security systems.
- Defence manufacturing has become an industrial system with potential civilian spillovers, while agriculture remains a foreign-exchange anchor whose value increasingly depends on logistics and processing.
- Private investment depends heavily on allocation of war risk and credible security arrangements; ceasefire alone is not equivalent to an investment-grade environment.

## Research questions to carry forward
- What security arrangement is sufficient to crowd private capital into reconstruction?
- How durable is external budget financing and how does conditionality interact with domestic reform capacity?
- Which displaced populations return, where, and with what effects on labour, housing and regional inequality?
- Which wartime industrial and digital capabilities survive into a peacetime production model?
- Does EU integration produce a deeper European trade/production orientation while Black Sea geography remains decisive?
