# Cislunar service infrastructure

## Structural assessment

Cislunar operations are beginning to separate some support functions from individual missions and reorganize them as reusable services. The durable mechanism is not a self-sustaining lunar economy; it is the shift toward shared communications, navigation, timing, relay and interoperability layers that missions may eventually consume rather than reproduce independently.

This transition is institutionally driven as much as technically driven. Public agencies define architectures, standards, procurement and anchor demand; commercial providers increasingly supply parts of the service layer. The model remains dependent on public procurement continuity and has not yet demonstrated broad recurring non-anchor demand.

## Technical and industrial structure

The reusable stack has several distinct layers:

- orbital navigation and orbit-characterization demonstrations;
- coordinated multi-spacecraft operations;
- commercial communications and navigation relays;
- interoperability standards across providers and agencies;
- ground networks and mission operations;
- surface utilities, especially resilient power;
- launch, cargo, mobility, maintenance and spares;
- later-stage resource processing, construction and manufacturing.

Communications and navigation can mature before the broader lunar industrial base. This creates an important analytical distinction: technically viable infrastructure can precede economically self-sustaining infrastructure by a long period.

## Institutional structure

The emerging architecture resembles an anchor-customer market. Agencies can reduce uncertainty by funding demonstrations, specifying interoperable interfaces and buying services, while private providers own or operate more of the delivery layer. If standards become operational across multiple providers, mission designers may be able to substitute purchased common services for bespoke support systems.

The key institutional test is therefore not whether standards or procurement programs exist, but whether they produce reliable multi-provider interoperability, repeated service delivery and demand beyond a narrow set of public anchor programs.

## Dependencies and constraints

The main durable constraints are:

- surface power and energy storage;
- launch cadence and cargo logistics;
- recurring demand sufficient to support fixed infrastructure;
- interoperability across providers and agencies;
- surface mobility, maintenance and environmental resilience;
- procurement continuity and credible service-level commitments.

A relay network can be useful with a relatively small installed base. A broader lunar production system requires a much larger complement of power, logistics, maintenance, mobility, construction and resource-handling capabilities.

## Economy, labour and access

Near-term economic transmission remains primarily terrestrial: spacecraft manufacturing, navigation payloads, communications equipment, ground networks, autonomous-navigation software, power systems, robotics, launch services and specialized engineering.

The current labour channel is demand for systems engineering, RF and optical communications, navigation, autonomy, power electronics, lunar-environment engineering and operations. Shared services could lower fixed integration requirements for smaller missions, but broader access remains conditional on pricing, procurement rules, standards, export controls, insurance, launch availability and provider concentration.

## Open questions

- Will commercial lunar relays provide repeated service to multiple independent missions?
- Will interoperability work operationally across providers and agencies rather than remain a specification-level objective?
- Can surface power and logistics reach long-duration reliability quickly enough to expand the installed base of users?
- Does demand broaden beyond public anchor programs enough to support recurring service economics?
- Do future missions actually redesign around purchasing common services instead of carrying equivalent capabilities?
