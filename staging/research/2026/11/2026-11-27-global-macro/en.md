# AI equities rebound as industrial profits and credit exposures reveal a more concentrated global cycle

*Global Macro — 27 November 2026 | Information cutoff: 08:42 BRT (11:42 UTC)*

## Executive assessment

The new evidence is not Friday's recovery in technology shares alone. It is the simultaneous emergence of three measures of the same investment cycle: a sharp concentration of industrial profit growth in China, the Bank of England's quantified assessment of AI financing commitments, and the largest weekly net withdrawal from global equity funds in fourteen months. They measure different populations and periods, but together clarify why a strong industrial earnings report and a fragile financial market can coexist. [1] [2] [3] [4]

China's industrial profits increased 20.1% year on year in October, after 14.4% in September. The rebound is not evenly distributed. Meanwhile, the Bank of England estimates that the potential exposure of large cloud infrastructure firms, including contractual commitments outside their balance sheets, is slightly above US$4 trillion. That is neither realized debt nor expected losses. It identifies a financial transmission channel whose significance cannot be inferred from the price of a semiconductor share. [1] [2] [3]

The working assessment is that AI infrastructure demand can sustain the revenue of parts of global manufacturing even as investors become less willing to finance its future expansion. If that divergence lasts, the adjustment would appear first in new financing terms and project decisions, later in orders and employment. The inference is conditional: there is no verified aggregate cancellation of AI infrastructure investment in today's evidence.

## 1. China's profits are growing faster than revenue

The National Bureau of Statistics reported January–October industrial profits of CNY 6.737 trillion, a comparable-basis increase of 16.0%. Revenue increased 6.7%, operating costs 6.2%, and the profit-to-revenue ratio reached 5.69%, up 0.45 percentage points. The survey covers industrial enterprises with at least CNY 20 million in annual revenue from principal operations. It is not a census of all firms and does not measure household purchasing power. [1] [2]

The difference between profit and revenue growth suggests improved profitability in the surveyed population rather than a proportionate increase in final demand. Sector composition is essential: profits in computer, communication and other electronic equipment manufacturing approximately doubled over the first ten months, while furniture manufacturing profits contracted 52.4%. [2]

All three observations cover the same cumulative period, but measure different accounting aggregates; the values are not additive. The gap does not establish that AI caused the aggregate increase. It can also reflect input prices, sector weights, operating leverage and changes in product mix.

A manufacturer selling equipment to a data-centre supply chain receives revenue when a customer orders or accepts equipment; the ultimate return on the data-centre project may take years to emerge. Rising supplier profits thus need not prove that the buyer's future investment is economically viable. Conversely, a decline in the buyer's equity valuation does not erase existing purchase commitments. The lag between these two balance sheets is the central issue.

## 2. AI financing now extends well beyond equity prices

The Bank of England's Financial Stability Report, released on 26 November, identifies an expanding network of bonds, private credit, leases, guarantees and joint ventures. It projects hyperscaler bond issuance above US$305 billion in 2026, approximately 8% of worldwide investment-grade issuance. Its broader potential-exposure estimate exceeds US$4 trillion when debt and off-balance-sheet contractual obligations are considered. These figures have different definitions and must not be added or used to calculate a leverage ratio. [3]

A long-term lease or purchase commitment may provide a data-centre operator with the expected cash flows needed to obtain financing. That arrangement can be commercially rational: the party using the capacity avoids some direct construction risk, and investors fund an asset against contracted revenues. The structure becomes more vulnerable if several projects depend on the same customers, whose ability to pay in turn depends on uncertain future AI revenues.

```flow
Expected demand for AI services
→ cloud capacity contracts and purchase commitments
→ debt and private financing for infrastructure
→ orders for chips, electrical systems and construction
→ supplier revenue and industrial profits

Lower expected returns or higher refinancing costs
→ tighter underwriting and revised project economics
→ delayed new capacity and equipment orders
→ slower supplier growth and possible employment effects
```

These are conditional transmission mechanisms, not recorded defaults. The Bank also identifies a widening spread for some weaker borrowers and more selective private-credit financing. That differentiation is analytically more useful than treating all AI-related issuers as a single credit category. A cash-generative platform can continue investing while a highly leveraged operator reliant on refinancing reduces new construction.

## 3. Market recovery is not yet a broad return of capital

The morning rebound in US equity futures and European and Asian shares followed the Thanksgiving closure of Wall Street. It was an intraday observation, not a completed US cash-market session. Reuters reported a rise of 2.8% in Nasdaq futures and 2.0% in S&P 500 futures. [5]

The fund-flow evidence published this morning describes an earlier interval, the week ending 25 November. LSEG Lipper's sample recorded net equity redemptions globally of US$22.73 billion. US equity funds lost US$30.57 billion; European funds gained US$7.29 billion. Global bond funds lost US$3.51 billion while government-bond funds attracted US$7.14 billion. These are fund subscriptions and redemptions, not all securities transactions or balance-of-payments flows. [4]

The global observation is an aggregate and overlaps the regional categories; it must not be summed with them. The regional categories shown are not exhaustive. The combination is consistent with portfolio differentiation and reduced exposure to selected risk assets, rather than a uniform withdrawal from every market. It does not demonstrate that the same investors moved directly from US to European shares.

The immediate test is whether the rally is followed by sustained subscriptions, lower credit spreads for the weaker borrowers and a recovery in new project financing. An equity price can rise quickly as its discount rate or expectations change; commitments for a new data centre are slower and more difficult to reverse.

Tokyo's inflation excluding fresh food accelerated as utility subsidies expired, but the measure also excluding energy eased. Spain's headline and core estimates moved lower. A simultaneous change in opposite directions is compatible with different energy contracts, fiscal support and base effects; it does not imply different movements in a common global demand indicator.

The US backdrop remains restrictive: October PCE inflation was 3.8% year on year and core PCE 3.3%, while real consumer expenditure was essentially unchanged on the month. Q3 real GDP nonetheless grew 4.3% at an annualized rate. [10] [11] The quarterly and monthly measures are not directly comparable. Monetary authorities can face persistent price growth even when financing for some investment projects becomes less available.

At 09:35 UTC, Brent futures were quoted at US$97.30 a barrel, up on the day amid renewed concerns about Middle Eastern supply. Reuters attributed reports of interruptions to regional sources and official statements. This edition does not independently establish the scale of any physical loss. [9] An extended disruption would raise import and transport costs, reduce real purchasing power for net importers and make inflation relief less reliable. A price increase without a lasting decline in deliverable barrels could instead reverse.

## 5. Scenarios and falsification

The central scenario is selective financing rather than a general collapse of the technology investment cycle. Established firms continue contracted spending, while projects with uncertain cash flows face higher financing hurdles. Supplier profit growth can remain strong temporarily as existing orders are delivered.

The adverse scenario requires further evidence: rising credit spreads followed by failed refinancing, reduced commitments and weaker equipment orders. If accompanied by sustained oil supply losses, the combination would reduce central banks' room to offset weaker investment. Countries dependent on equipment exports or imported fuel would face different but potentially simultaneous pressures.

The alternative is durable improvement in expected AI returns. Rising paid usage and cash generation could validate existing commitments, draw capital back into funds and extend investment. A narrowing of credit spreads, broadening industrial profitability and consistent growth in non-technology demand would weaken the concentration thesis.

The next observations should be actual project financing and cancellation disclosures; new orders and supplier inventories; November fund flows and sector spreads; final Spanish inflation, nationwide Japanese inflation and the Bank of Japan's December decision; and Gulf export volumes and shipping data. A holiday-period futures move should not substitute for these tests.

**Confidence:** medium for the concentration and transmission assessment; lower for the timing and magnitude of any slowdown. The analysis uses accounting aggregates, sampled fund flows and regulatory estimates of contractual exposure. It is not a forecast of defaults, a synchronized global market close, or proof of causal identification.
