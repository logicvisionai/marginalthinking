# Global Wealth Weekly — Cash absorbs record inflows while equity funds attract money despite falling share prices

**9 November 2026 · Weekly allocation and ownership monitor · Cutoff: early Monday Brasília time.** The principal new flow evidence covers the **week ending 4 November**, reported by LSEG Lipper on 6 November; US Investment Company Institute (ICI) money-market assets also refer to **4 November**, while ICI combined long-term mutual-fund/ETF figures end **28 October**. Gold ETF statistics cover **October**, BIS bank credit and IMF currency reserves cover **2026 Q2**. These windows must remain separate: a new Monday price is not a new weekly transaction series. [1][2][3][5][6][7]

Global equity funds received **US$38.63 billion** of net purchases in the week through 4 November, their seventh consecutive positive week. Global bond funds attracted **US$9.91 billion**, but the largest flow was into global money-market funds: **US$172.71 billion**, the strongest weekly figure since April 2020 in LSEG Lipper's universe. That coincided with a sharp equity correction in technology and Asian stock markets. Investors can subscribe to pooled equity funds while equity indices decline: one is a quantity of cash going into fund vehicles, the other is a repricing of stocks already outstanding. They are not competing measurements of the same thing. [1]

The relative scale of money-market subscriptions is informative, but it does not mean the world destroyed equities to create cash. Some investors may be raising liquidity, treasurers may be moving corporate balances, institutions may be changing fund wrappers, and new savings may enter simultaneously. The reporting source does not identify each ultimate household or company. A security sale by one portfolio often simply transfers ownership to another; the global stock of net financial assets cannot be inferred by summing one fund universe's subscriptions. [1][2]

## 2. Geography: allocations do not track regional equity performance one for one

European equity funds received **US$13.25 billion** of net subscriptions, US equity funds **US$14.81 billion**, and Asian equity funds **US$8.34 billion** during the week through 4 November. Although the US and major Asian equity benchmarks sold off during the subsequent turbulent period, the flows remained positive in the sampled week. Overlapping geographic classifications, missing fund coverage and date mismatches prevent adding these figures to a worldwide capital-account balance. [1]

```chart
type: bar
title: LSEG Lipper equity fund flows by major region, week to 4 November
unit: US dollars, billions; geographic subcategories, not world total
United States | 14.81
Europe | 13.25
Asia | 8.34
```

Price and flow can point in opposite directions for structural reasons. A fund can receive subscriptions and purchase listed securities from other holders, yet the market price falls if sellers require a lower clearing price. Conversely, higher prices can reflect discount-rate changes even without net purchases by fund investors. International equity returns also contain exchange-rate effects for investors measuring in another currency, while reported USD subscriptions are cash-flow conversions under the data provider's methodology.

Europe's large subscription reading fits a possible diversification or lower energy-risk thesis, but that remains interpretation. The euro-area's high energy inflation, country-specific fiscal constraints and renewed debate about rates mean a European allocation is not uniformly safer. Asian fund purchases likewise should not be mistaken for evidence that AI equipment factories immediately expanded output. A genuine claim of productive relocation requires orders, investment spending, trade volumes and ownership records.

Emerging-market equity funds received **US$1.91 billion**, their fifth consecutive positive week, while emerging-market bond funds lost **US$299.07 million** in the Lipper sample. A positive emerging equity reading does not establish that sovereign financing improved: equity-fund investors and bondholders face different currency, duration and credit risks. This divergence is especially relevant when dollar yields rise and import-dependent emerging economies confront volatile energy delivery costs. [1]

## 3. Cash allocation: why the ICI and Lipper numbers must not be summed

The ICI reported **US$8,015.74 billion** of US money-market fund assets for the week ended 4 November, up **US$114.46 billion** from **US$7,901.28 billion** on 28 October. Institutional assets rose **US$102.25 billion** and retail assets **US$12.21 billion**. The government-fund segment expanded **US$117.80 billion**; prime funds declined **US$4.03 billion**. Components may differ from the published total slightly because of rounding and definitional treatment. [2]

```chart
type: bar
title: ICI US money-market assets across two reporting weeks
unit: US dollars, billions; end-of-week asset stocks
28 October | 7901.28
4 November | 8015.74
```

Asset changes are primarily driven by investor flows and can be used as a proxy for net new cash, according to the ICI. They are not identical to a fully audited transactional flow: market valuation, accruals, distribution and instrument reclassification may also contribute. The **US$114.46 billion** asset-stock change is within ICI's US money-market universe, whereas Lipper's **US$172.71 billion** is a flow estimate for global money-market funds. Adding them would double-count overlap and mix a weekly stock movement with flow classification.

Government money-market funds increased to **US$6,622.38 billion**, including **US$4,635.21 billion** of institutional government-fund assets. This shows the importance of institutional liquid-asset management but does not prove that government debt was purchased in equivalent new net issuance. Secondary-market transactions, bank deposits, repo transactions, Treasury bills and fund reserve practices all mediate where each dollar is ultimately placed. The identity of the issuer and the legal structure of the fund matter for collateral and rollover risk. [2]

```flow
Investor and corporate cash balances → Subscriptions to money-market vehicles → Portfolio purchases of bills, repos and other short instruments
Existing securities sold by one holder → Purchased by another holder → Change in ownership, not automatically new aggregate wealth
Equity multiple compression → Market value loss of existing shares → Changes in collateral and financing conditions, even without net fund outflows
Gold ETF new units and bullion acquisitions → Higher physical holdings → Can coexist with lower asset value when bullion price falls
```

The flow diagram identifies possible transmission channels rather than assuming a one-to-one allocation of specific fund subscriptions into newly issued public debt. The balance-sheet counterparties must be observed before the destination of each flow is asserted.

## 4. Longer-term vehicles: recent ICI data revise the extraordinary October narrative

ICI's combined US long-term mutual fund and ETF series recorded **US$9.36 billion** of net positive flows for the week ended **28 October**, after **US$12.324 billion** the week ended 21 October. The more recent week combined **US$36.55 billion** of mutual-fund outflows with **US$45.91 billion** of ETF net issuance. ETF net issuance is already included in the combined total; adding it again would double-count. This is a different investment population from the worldwide Lipper equity total for 4 November. [3][4]

The preceding 14 October combined estimate was an extraordinary negative **US$471.677 billion** reading linked primarily to collective investment trust activity. As the previous Global Wealth edition documented, the ICI methodology cautions against treating that accounting/institutional movement as proof that retail households collectively sold nearly half a trillion dollars of equity. The fund wrapper, redemption instruction, custody and underlying economic owner's exposure may change in different ways.

For 28 October, the combined ICI category figures included **US$11.074 billion** in equities, **US$3.166 billion** in bonds and **-US$2.994 billion** in hybrid funds. These category observations reveal allocation preference inside the sampled vehicles. They do not quantify net international capital exports, because a US-listed fund can buy foreign securities, an overseas investor can buy US shares, and the same shares may change hands without a new corporate investment. [3]

The practical distinction is between portfolio allocation, corporate capital raising and real productive investment. Purchasing a previously issued share in the secondary market transfers that security, while a new share issuance can provide funds to the issuer. A direct greenfield investment builds productive capacity only when expenditure actually finances construction, equipment, employment and commissioned projects. M&A changes control only after the legal and transaction conditions are satisfied; an announcement does not do so.

## 5. Gold: physical holdings rise while dollar-denominated assets fall

The World Gold Council reported **US$2.7 billion** of global physically backed gold ETF net inflows in **October 2026**. ETF holdings increased **19 tonnes to 4,275 tonnes**, a month-end record in its series. Yet aggregate assets under management declined about **6% to US$542 billion** because the gold price fell. This is unusually clear evidence of the distinction between physical holdings, investor subscriptions and market valuation. [5]

European gold vehicles led the monthly inflow, with Asian investors also net buyers and North America showing small outflows. Year to date through October, net gold ETF inflows were **US$41 billion**, accompanied by **246 tonnes** more holdings. These values concern exchange-traded investment products, not official central-bank bullion holdings or newly mined gold. A central-bank purchase would appear in another reserve and monetary-gold accounting family. [5][9]

```chart
type: bar
title: World Gold Council global ETF bullion holdings
unit: metric tonnes, month-end; comparable physical inventory
September 2026 | 4256
October 2026 | 4275
```

A six-percent decline in AUM cannot be reinterpreted as a six-percent withdrawal of gold from vaults when tonnes rose. It primarily reflects price valuation. Similarly, higher tonnes do not establish who ultimately controlled each bar because holdings are inside investment vehicles with legal custody and shareholder structures. Economic exposure to gold can also be obtained through derivatives that do not directly increase fund-backed physical holdings.

The Council expects continued investment demand in late 2026 but recognizes competing forces: tighter policy, higher bond yields and a stronger dollar can pressure gold prices, while geopolitical uncertainty and financial stress can favor demand for hedges. An outlook is not a realized future allocation, and its assumptions must be revisited when inflation and rate data change. [9]

## 6. International banking and reserve currency composition: different clocks

The BIS report released in late October estimated **US$40.7 trillion** of cross-border bank credit outstanding at end-June, after **US$877 billion** of adjusted growth in Q2. Of that increase **US$424 billion** was euro-denominated and **US$383 billion** dollar-denominated, with approximately **US$70 billion** in other currencies. Both euro and dollar credit segments grew **11%** year on year. The evidence suggests rising relative importance of the euro in some new funding, not an abrupt disappearance of dollar lending. [6]

Bank credit is a claim of the lender and a liability of the borrower. A new bank loan expands both sides of balance sheets; it cannot be counted as additional consolidated world net wealth. The BIS distinguishes broader cross-border claims from narrower credit consisting of loans and debt securities. The Q2 claims increase of **US$1.3 trillion** overlaps the **US$877 billion** credit number, so the two are not additive. Country exposures require currency, counterparty and final borrower identification. [6]

IMF COFER showed **US$13.22 trillion** of foreign-exchange reserve assets in Q2, versus **US$13.10 trillion** in Q1. The dollar share fell from **57.18% to 56.70%**, while the euro's increased from **20.04% to 20.60%**. The IMF described euro reserve claims rising nearly US$100 billion and attributed much of the change to active buying. These estimates describe official monetary reserves; gold, SDRs and the reserve position in the IMF are outside this specific COFER currency universe. [7][8]

```chart
type: bar
title: IMF Q2 2026 share of reported world FX reserves
unit: percent of COFER official foreign exchange reserves
US dollar | 56.70
Euro | 20.60
```

COFER shares and BIS borrowing currency growth measure different entities, reference dates and accounting concepts. A euro liability outside Europe does not require a euro purchase by an official monetary authority. Neither time series alone supports a comprehensive verdict on dollar dominance in payments, debt contracting, collateral and security markets. The November 2026 portfolio-fund data cannot be merged with Q2 reserve shares to calculate a single monthly de-dollarisation flow.

## 7. Equity turbulence, industrial finance and real ownership

The week spanning the US midterms included sharp selling of technology shares, especially semiconductor names, followed by a partial Friday bounce. On Monday morning in Asia, some equity markets recovered. These price changes can alter the financing terms of AI infrastructure developers and suppliers, yet must be distinguished from actual project commissioning. An index rebound does not guarantee recovery in private data-center valuations, and an index decline does not prove that grid orders were canceled. [11][12]

For corporate ownership, a transfer requires identifiable legal rights, settlement, voting interests or control mechanisms. A fall in the market capitalisation of a widely held company is a revaluation of already held equity, not a transfer of the whole company to its counterparties. For sovereign debt, rising yields generally reduce prices of outstanding fixed-rate bonds; when new debt is issued, the issuer's actual financing terms depend on the auction, maturity and currency. A purchase of existing debt by a fund does not itself finance a new public project.

Two distinct types of financial fragility deserve monitoring. **Funding fragility** appears when companies cannot refinance near-term liabilities or issue new capital without severe dilution. **Valuation fragility** appears when portfolios mark down long-duration future profits without immediate cash-flow insolvency. Both can coexist, but policy and investment responses differ. Separating them requires credit spreads, refinancing schedules, issuance volumes and realized project expenditure—not market slogans about money leaving technology.

Infrastructure and energy supply also create transmission to the physical economy. A higher delivered fuel cost can impair airline or industrial margins, whereas power-generation and grid construction may depend on multi-year contractual cash flows. The global concentration of AI-related investment could increase demand for specialized components even while a particular technology fund's subscriptions fall. Only observed orders and construction drawdowns establish that productive investment is changing.

## 8. Regional transmission and unresolved claims of power

```map
United States | Large inflows to equity and money-market vehicles; 4 Nov ICI cash asset stock US$8.016tn | Different fund universes and weeks, not a national balance of payments
Europe | Lipper equity fund inflows US$13.25bn; October gold ETF demand leads | Fund exposure is not a legal transfer of industrial control
Asia | Equity fund inflows US$8.34bn despite technology market repricing | No inference about actual semiconductor plant output
Emerging economies | Equity fund inflows US$1.91bn and bond outflows US$0.299bn | Fund categories can overlap; credit risk and currency risk differ
Global monetary reserves | IMF COFER dollar share 56.70%, euro 20.60% in Q2 | Quarterly official asset shares, not November private capital flows
Global banking | BIS end-June credit stock US$40.7tn | Bank claims and liabilities do not measure consolidated world wealth
```

A qualitative region map identifies exposure and mechanisms rather than estimating unseen cross-border beneficial ownership. Claims of global political influence need more than capital market allocations. They require evidence of financing dependence, legal veto rights, critical physical infrastructure, sanctions exposure or access to essential technology. Large asset managers may exercise voting rights on behalf of fund beneficiaries without personally owning the underlying assets.

This distinction also affects conclusions about currencies. An economy can borrow more in euros, hold reserves partly in dollars, invoice trade in another currency and use dollar collateral in global derivatives. Monetary power resides in those linked institutional arrangements, not one percentage alone. Any significant change should be traced through contracts, reserve transactions, payment rails and funding resilience rather than a single weekly fund-flow table.

## 9. Falsifiable paths and next releases

| Conditional interpretation | Confirmation | Disconfirmation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Liquid portfolios diversify while keeping money-market buffers | Continued equity and government-fund inflows with stable credit spreads and issuance | Abrupt fund redemptions, sharply higher spreads and failed financing |
| AI valuation reset spreads into real investment | Canceled data-center contracts, lower equipment orders and credit rationing | Continued capex execution, new financing and stable supplier backlog |
| Euro gains in some reserve and credit segments persist | Consistent subsequent COFER allocations and BIS currency-adjusted growth | Reserve reallocations reverse or dollar borrowing regains share |
| Energy turbulence drives defensive liquidity demand | Freight/insurance premiums rise with verifiable delivery interruptions and money-market inflows | Safer shipping, cheaper delivered fuel and normalized short-term funding |

The coming US October CPI release on **10 November** is a direct inflation test that can shift interest-rate expectations and bond valuations. It is not yet observed. Subsequent ICI weekly reports, the next Lipper sample, official Treasury TIC transactions and ETF bullion holdings will be more informative about actual portfolio and currency changes than the Monday opening of one stock market. [2][3][10][11]

The reported allocation regime is not a broad liquidation of stocks. It combines positive equity-fund subscriptions, extraordinarily large demand for liquid money-market vehicles, continued bond-fund inflows and reduced risk appetite in selected technology markets. The deeper wealth question remains the allocation of legal claims, liquidity and control—not a simplistic claim that falling stock prices imply that cash vanished or changed sovereign ownership.
