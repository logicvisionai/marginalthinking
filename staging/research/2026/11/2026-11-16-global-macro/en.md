# Global Macro — China’s factories expand, Japanese consumption falls and the dollar raises adjustment costs

**16 November 2026 · Information cutoff at 07:55 Brasília time (10:55 UTC).** China and Japan released their data on Monday in Asia. The dollar and crude oil readings are intraday observations. US Treasury yields are from Friday, 13 November, the latest published session. [1][2][4][8][9]

China started the week with high-tech industrial value added **17.6%** above October 2025 and nominal retail sales up just **0.4%**. Japan reported essentially flat real GDP in the third quarter. Private consumption fell **0.3%** from the previous quarter while private non-residential investment rose **1.6%**. Both releases show segments still able to invest or increase output even as households contribute little momentum to demand. The statistics, periods and mechanisms differ across the two economies. [1][2][3]

The composition matters for Brazil. An Asian machinery order can sustain suppliers of particular components and inputs even as demand for construction materials weakens and local credit grows more expensive. The dollar rose in Asian trading on Monday morning and Brent eased. Together, those moves illustrate why a lower dollar-denominated commodity price can deliver only partial relief to a Brazilian business that imports, holds inventory and borrows locally. [8][9][11]

## 2. China’s decline in investment shifts demand between suppliers

```chart

Industry above designated size | 4.8

```

Industrial output monitored by the NBS rose **0.21%** from September on the monthly series. Equipment and high-tech manufacturing outgrew overall industry on the annual comparison. The manufacturing purchasing managers’ index printed **49.3**, below the dividing line between improving and deteriorating conditions in the diffusion measure. The output series weights the value produced. The survey summarizes the direction of responses. Aggregate output can rise when expansion is concentrated in some activities and business conditions are weaker across the survey sample. [1][7]

Fixed-asset investment fell **8.1%** in January to October, and fell **5.1%** after excluding real-estate development. Property explains part of the gap between the two measures. The measure excluding property points to wider weakness. This shift changes the inputs used in investment. A housing development calls for steel, cement, construction equipment and local labor in different proportions from an electronics plant. Replacing one project with the other can sustain some manufacturing businesses while reducing orders for distributors and suppliers serving construction. [1]

China’s nominal goods exports in local currency rose **28.7%** from October 2025 and imports rose **34.7%**. Prices, volumes and product mix all enter these figures. A larger import bill can reflect higher oil prices, input purchases for further processing or stronger final household demand. Disentangling these requires quantities by product and evidence about how the imports are used. For Brazilian iron ore, the relevant tests are steel output, ore import volumes and stocks at Chinese ports. A strong order book for electronics equipment, in isolation, tells little about those three variables. [1][7]


Japanese GDP was close enough to zero for the Cabinet Office’s quarterly table to round the change to **-0.0%**. Reuters reported an annualized **-0.1%**. Domestic demand contributed **+0.6 percentage points** and net exports contributed **-0.6 percentage points**, both rounded. Rising imports account for most of the external drag. Some household, business and government spending purchases output from abroad. GDP accounting subtracts that portion to measure production within Japan. The aggregate release leaves open how much of the import increase went into inventories and how much went into final use. [2][3]

The gap between business investment and consumption shapes the Bank of Japan’s choices. Households face prevailing prices for goods and services, while investment can reflect contracts and decisions from several months earlier. A policy response based solely on headline GDP would miss that difference in timing. Wages, services prices and the extent to which the exchange rate passes through to food, energy and other imported costs also matter. In Asian trading Reuters recorded the dollar around **159.59 yen**. The spot price describes market conditions at that moment. Its implications for future Japanese prices depend on pass-through. [2][3][8]

## 4. The United States and Europe: income, credit and the cost of investment

The Fed held its target range at **3.75% to 4.00%** on 28 October, with two votes in favor of a 25-basis-point increase. US consumer prices rose **0.4%** in October from September and core prices rose **0.3%**, both seasonally adjusted. Friday’s two-year Treasury yield was **4.88%** and the ten-year yield was **5.15%**. The combination includes positive monthly inflation, a recorded split on the committee and long yields above the current target range. Longer yields also reflect expectations and compensation for holding bonds over longer periods. The minutes scheduled for 18 November will give more detail about the arguments at the October meeting. [4][5][6][14]

Reuters reported caution across global equities ahead of Nvidia’s earnings. A higher cost of capital has more impact on the present value of projects expected to generate revenue years from now. Realized revenue, customer commitments, construction schedules and investment actually paid out help distinguish demand for computing capacity from financial valuations. When orders remain firm as financing tightens, firms able to fund projects with their own cash may gain an advantage. Small suppliers that borrow to fund equipment and working capital face similar orders with less room to absorb tighter terms. [4][10]

Euro-area GDP rose **0.4%** in the third quarter, below **0.6%** in the previous quarter, and employment was flat in the flash estimate. With output rising and headcount steady, hours worked and sector composition matter for interpreting productivity and income. Aggregate growth can coexist with weakness among businesses exposed to more competitive Asian imports. If Chinese producers redirect supply to Europe, the impact would likely first appear in exposed segments and import prices. Energy, wages and domestic services follow their own paths. [1][13]

The APEC leaders’ meeting in Shenzhen puts trade policy back on this week’s agenda. Reuters reported an expected meeting between the US and Chinese presidents. The effect on trade depends on measures that change the cost of bringing goods into a market or buyers’ ability to contract with suppliers. A bilateral announcement can change expectations immediately. The impact on shipments depends on the text, which products it covers, when it takes effect and how purchasers respond. That sequence helps distinguish new orders driven by final demand from purchases brought forward ahead of a tariff change. [10][17]

## 5. Brazil’s exposure through energy, exchange rates and credit

```flow
Recovering crude exports → pressure on dollar prices → exchange-rate-dependent cost in reais → impact on freight and margins
Elevated Treasury yields and firm dollar → potentially higher funding and hedging costs → borrowing conditions for inventories and investment
```

```map
title: Regional exposures to the changing composition of demand
China | High-tech output outpaces industry as a whole | Retail sales and property investment point to an uneven domestic recovery
Japan | Firms invest as private consumption falls | Imports weigh on the external contribution and the exchange rate affects costs
United States and euro area | Expensive US financing and slowing euro-area growth | Debt maturity, import origin and service-sector weight shape transmission
Brazil | Exporters, importers and households absorb different shocks | Shipment volumes, the dollar in reais and borrowing rates need separate observation
```

Brent eased to **US$95.15** per barrel at 09:19 UTC. The International Energy Agency estimated that global oil supply reached **105.4 million barrels per day** in October and projected a fourth-quarter surplus. The outcome depends on Gulf shipments and demand. Shipping security, insurance premiums and diesel availability determine how much of a fall in crude prices can reach freight costs. A 1% decline in the dollar price and a 1% rise in the price of the dollar in reais nearly offset each other in the multiplication before taxes, margins and logistics costs. This is an arithmetic sensitivity example. [9][15]

Brazil’s Copom kept the Selic rate at **13.75%** on 4 November. October IPCA inflation was **0.14%** on the month, but food and beverages rose **1.04%**. The difference weighs more on the budgets of households that spend a larger share of income on food. A stronger dollar can raise export revenue per unit in reais, while lower prices or volumes can reduce total sales. Importers face replacement costs in reais and must account for how fast stocks turn over. Businesses that sell on credit also face their own borrowing costs and customers’ ability to pay. The Focus survey and September IBC-Br were scheduled for after the cutoff. Focus captures private expectations, and IBC-Br measures economic activity under its own methodology. [11][12][16]

## 6. Scenarios for the week

| Scenario | Evidence that strengthens it | Evidence that weakens it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrow industrial expansion and weak Asian domestic demand | Resilient technology output, soft Chinese retail sales and no rebound in Japanese consumption | A joint improvement in sales, real income and investment outside leading segments |
| Cheaper crude brings partial relief in local currency | More Gulf shipments, lower Brent and a still-firm dollar | Shipment disruptions or sufficient dollar weakening to extend the relief |
| Dollar funding remains a constraint | Elevated long yields alongside higher private funding costs | A sustained decline in benchmark yields and spreads for comparable borrowers |

Confidence is **high** in the cited official readings and **moderate** in the transmission assessment. Oil, currency and equity moves over a single morning offer little causal evidence. A broad improvement in consumption, investment beyond the leading industries and private credit would change the assessment more than a rise in a single stock index. For Brazil, weaker activity accompanied by higher inflation expectations would imply a different policy constraint from weaker activity alongside falling expectations. [1][2][4][10][15][16]
