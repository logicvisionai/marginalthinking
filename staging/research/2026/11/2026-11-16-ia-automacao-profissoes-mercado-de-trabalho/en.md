# AI Arrived Before the Labour Market Could Adjust

In October 2026, US software-development postings on Indeed were 23.8% higher than a year earlier. They were still 19.2% below February 2020. The recovery also had a revealing composition. In a different window, May 2025 to May 2026, senior roles accounted for 71% of the net gain in software postings on the platform. All three observations fit the same account. A firm can resume investment in engineering and need more experienced developers while offering fewer opportunities to people looking for a first job. [1, 2]

Adjustment can begin long before the first dismissal. An accounting practice that automates some bookkeeping can leave a departing assistant's position unfilled. An agency that produces twenty versions of a campaign on the budget for five may buy less work from a studio. A publisher that commissions post-editing in place of a full translation changes the relationship between deadlines, responsibility and pay. Total employment may barely move while contracts arrive less often, entrants earn differently and the work through which beginners gain experience changes.

A measurable claim about the speed of adjustment has three parts. A tool must first lower the cost of an accepted output, including verification, integration and mistakes. A buyer or employer must then act on the saving through prices, volumes, hiring or the allocation of responsibility. Finally, affected workers must be able to reach new opportunities within a period they can afford. A firm can postpone a vacancy within one budget cycle. A worker may need years to accumulate experience recognised in a different role. The timing difference is best documented so far in early-career US employment and parts of commissioned digital work. Its extent across entire economies, including Brazil, remains uncertain. [3–9]

## Task automation meets a labour market organised around occupations

Computerisation showed that a job contains tasks with different opportunities for substitution. Autor, Levy and Murnane documented a relative decline in routine manual and cognitive activities and an increase in tasks involving, among other capabilities, problem solving and interaction. Generative AI expands the exposed set. A system can draft original prose, produce an image from a description or propose an implementation without a programmer having specified a procedure for that operation in advance. The task can feel unfamiliar to the person doing it and still contain enough regularity for the model to help. [10]

The trajectory of an occupation also depends on demand. In the framework developed by Acemoglu and Restrepo, substitution displaces labour from tasks taken up by capital. An increase in production and the creation of new tasks can restore demand for labour. Their relative speeds matter. New jobs at large firms are unlikely to replace, in the same quarter, the dispersed commissions that supported independent workers. A newly created review task can also go to someone already employed by the adopting firm, leaving the former supplier without a route into the new work. [11]

Consider a service that used to take ten labour hours per unit. If it takes seven after adoption, supplying the same quantity needs 30% fewer hours. Production must rise from one hundred to about 143 units to preserve total hours at the new labour intensity. At 115 units, hours fall from one thousand to 805. At 170, they rise to 1,190. The calculation separates the performance of a tool from the response of buyers. Unmet demand, such as a service with a long waiting list, can allow substantial expansion. Where customers already buy as much as they need, an equivalent gain can reduce billed hours and revenue.

Payment terms also change the outcome. A salaried worker may deliver more in the same workweek for the same pay. A freelancer who charges by the hour can lose revenue per order even at an unchanged hourly rate. On a fixed-price project, someone who works faster keeps the immediate benefit while the fee, quality threshold and quantity remain the same. Buyers can later revise the fee after recognising the saving. If higher output creates additional supervision, contingency work or responsibility for errors, earnings may stay stable while workers lose discretion over how they spend their time. Adoption studies need to track turnaround time, revenue and time worked individually.

The ILO estimates that about a quarter of workers worldwide are in occupations with some exposure to generative AI. The highest exposure group represents 3.3% of global employment. Some degree of exposure applies to 11% of employment in low-income countries and 34% in high-income economies. These estimates concern typical occupational tasks. Deployment depends on digitisation, institutional organisation, data and operational capacity. Anthropic also finds a wide gap between tasks models might help perform and uses observed on Claude, which covers one provider's customers. [12, 13]

A US Census Bureau business survey covering November 2025 to January 2026 found that 18% of firms used AI in a business function. The employment-weighted figure was 32%, reflecting higher adoption among large firms. Among AI users, 2% reported an AI-related fall in headcount. The figure measures what firms reported during that specific window. A hiring delay can escape a question about realised staffing reductions. In Denmark, linked administrative records show task reorganisation and near-zero estimated effects on earnings and recorded hours in the first two years of chatbot diffusion. The ILO's November 2026 assessment similarly describes modest aggregate effects and uneven sectoral change. [7–9]

```flow
Lower task cost → verification and integration determine savings per output → volume and prices respond → firms change hours, contracts and vacancies → workers enter the transition with different incomes and qualifications
```

## Application programmers, software engineers and the first job

### Application programmers

A junior assignment might involve building a form, adding a validation rule and writing the required tests. The code has to satisfy the business rule, respect the repository's conventions and fail intelligibly. AI shortens research and implementation when the specification is clear and familiar. In a system governed by undocumented historical rules, the new burden can be reconstructing intentions, checking the authorisation for an operation and establishing what changes for existing users.

Across three field experiments involving 4,867 developers, Cui and colleagues estimated 26.1% more completed tasks with the AI tool studied. Less experienced developers adopted it more readily and recorded larger gains. METR studied a different setting. Sixteen experienced contributors completed 246 tasks in mature repositories they knew well. With early-2025 tools, tasks took 19% longer. A February 2026 update found signs of improvement but substantial selection problems. Some developers stopped contributing tasks precisely because they preferred to do them with AI. These studies measure different tools, workers and completion criteria. Averaging the percentages would lose the information about when each effect occurred. [14, 15]

Higher junior productivity gives employers a choice. A firm can bring more juniors into a team and use the tool to reduce part of its training cost. An experienced developer can also complete the assignments that once went to a junior. The choice depends on supervision costs, demand and the expected return on training future mid-career staff. Hiring creates a commitment of months or years. Software subscriptions and usage can rise with less commitment.

### Software engineers

Writing code and delivering it to users require different kinds of capacity. A change needs specification, editing, review, testing, deployment and monitoring. A 2026 study covering more than 500,000 GitHub developers estimated that adoption of autonomous agents increased commits by a cumulative 240%, the number of projects by 80% and releases by 30%. The matched event study compares adopters with controls and measures observable activity. Commit counts can rise when work is divided into smaller changes. Releases are closer to output, with usage, revenue, reliability and maintenance still to be measured. The researchers also found an increase in app supply across marketplaces without a corresponding increase in total usage. [16]

The binding constraint can move from implementation to the people who understand data contracts, prioritise changes and take the call when a deployment fails. Rushing review imposes a cost of its own. A management system that rewards code production can record an improvement while verification accumulates in someone else's queue. Time to production, defects encountered by users and maintenance effort bring hiring decisions closer to what customers actually buy.

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects a 7.3% fall in employment of *computer programmers* and a 10.2% rise for *software developers* from 2025 to 2035. US statistical categories distinguish job content that can appear under similar titles in Brazilian firms. The BLS projection incorporates several technological and economic assumptions. It helps identify where occupational composition might change. The 2025–2026 increase in senior software postings needs to be read alongside actual junior hiring, starting pay and time to find a first position. [1, 2, 17]

### Web developers

Web development covers fixed-price sites of a few pages and applications with thousands of clients, payments and integrations. In the first market, a customer can buy a template, use a hosted site builder or generate a prototype internally. Site builders already competed for this work. AI changes the cost of tailoring the result, editing content and attempting repairs without going back to an agency. The adjustment can show up first in fixed-price bids and one-off changes.

An application that links inventory, payments and internal rules sells continuity. The customer pays to fix problems in older versions, manage data and adapt the system as the business changes. Rapid prototyping can bring more proposals into a budget. Converting them into jobs depends on how many products reach production and remain in use. Aggregating brochure websites and transactional systems under one title can conceal movement in opposite directions.

### Quality assurance analysts and testers

Generating tests lowers the cost of preparing repetitive cases and extends the number a team can run. The harder work is selecting conditions that reveal faults and checking that the tests reflect a real requirement. A suite based on the same mistaken interpretation of the code can increase measured coverage while leaving a defect untested. Customers experience the defect even when the coverage dashboard improves.

Automation can reduce repetitive manual execution in entry-level jobs. An increase in releases may raise demand for exploratory testing and acceptance checks. The relevant measures include QA hours per release, defects found in use and the allocation of staff between repetitive and analytical work. US growth projections for software testers and QA analysts are compatible with sustained demand for verification as parts of execution become easier. [17]

### Data and business intelligence analysts

A SQL query is inexpensive when the meaning of the metric is settled. Costs rise when offices define an active customer differently, cancelled orders are reclassified or several tables record the same event. A model makes it easier to query data and generate a convincing explanation. It also makes a plausible competing calculation easier to produce. Time saved compiling reports can pay for a metric catalogue, better records of data provenance and consultation with operational teams.

A provider of standardised dashboards faces conditions similar to a supplier of basic websites. A business trying to determine whether revenue grew because of price, volume or an accounting change needs shared definitions and validation. Pay can rise for people who understand the business and are accountable for its data while hiring falls for repetitive spreadsheet and reporting work. Aggregate counts under the analyst title would miss that shift.

### Systems administrators, infrastructure staff and technical support

In infrastructure, assistants can help draft documentation, inspect logs and prepare routine changes. The rest of the work includes understanding impact, scheduling outages, restoring services and taking responsibility when a change fails. Technical support can automate instructions for predictable tickets. The remaining queue becomes smaller and harder. A target based on tickets per worker can then register an apparent productivity fall among the people left to handle exceptions.

The economics depend on whether a customer pays for availability or for a ticket. Standby staffing, redundancy and emergency recovery have value during long periods without an incident. A service provider paid by ticket can lose revenue as trivial requests disappear. The same system can lighten the work of the buyer's internal team and reduce outsourced hours.

### The training hidden inside production work

Reviewing a test, fixing a small integration and tracing the history of a defect are productive assignments and opportunities to learn. A firm can automate a first attempt and give the beginner supervised responsibility for verification. It can also give all verification to the experienced workers who already know the system. Quarterly delivery numbers might look similar while the two groups of beginners develop very different skills.

In a 2026 experiment on an asynchronous Python library, AI-assisted participants scored 17% lower on a subsequent assessment. The small time saving was statistically inconclusive. The duration and the type of exercise limit the result. The study also describes users who remained engaged by asking for explanations. Longer-term evaluation would measure independent performance, quality of review and progression months after assisted work. [19]

Training uses the time of experienced colleagues today. Its future benefit can accrue to another firm if the worker leaves. When each business tries to hire experienced people trained elsewhere, total investment in entry-level training can fall below the industry's needs. An extended shortage of junior roles could raise experienced pay, make departures harder to replace and concentrate difficult decisions in fewer people. Promotion rates, pay by experience and the share of senior staff grown internally can test that scenario.

## Designers, artists and the cost of approval

### Graphic designers

A retail campaign's appetite for images depends on its schedule, ad formats and the return from each creative. Generative tools reduce the cost of layouts, crops, background removal and variants. Moving from five assets to twenty can absorb some of the saving. Eventually an advertiser runs into limits on media spend, attention and the number of versions it can evaluate. A twentieth asset can be worth much less than the first five. Anyone who sold each variation separately faces a change in contract economics even if the buyer uses more images.

Demirci, Hannane and Zhu estimated a 17% drop in image-creation postings after the introduction of generative image tools on a freelance platform. They also found 21% fewer postings for exposed writing and coding work compared with categories intensive in manual skills. Platform categories and the study window define the estimates. Estimating the earnings of designers who remain in the market requires fees actually paid, contract duration, frequency and competition per job. [5]

A CHI 2026 experiment asked 36 participants to design ads with and without AI. Clients and external raters found assisted outputs more creative and unconventional. Perceived usefulness was similar, and clients reported no gain in brand alignment. The difference helps explain the value of finishing, checking brand fit and approval. An image-generation count per hour omits those parts of the service. [20]

### Interface designers

Generating screens is cheaper when components, navigation and error states can be drafted from a specification. Teams can turn more ideas into prototypes. A usable interface still needs decisions about devices, accessibility, data states and the consequences of each action. A polished checkout mock-up can lack a response to a declined payment, an expired session or a screen reader. A larger set of prototypes creates value when teams can choose and deliver better workflows.

A supplier selling individual screens may receive fewer manual assignments. An internal design team can spend the saving on earlier testing and hard-to-use flows. BLS's 6% growth projection for web and digital interface designers and 2% decline for graphic designers between 2025 and 2035 make the case for following the groups separately. Track approved flows, consistency and errors in use alongside hiring and team growth. [17, 18]

### User experience researchers

Generating transcripts and grouping responses takes time. AI can save some of it and retrieve overlooked observations from a large collection. The value of interview work depends on participant selection, the questions and the conditions in which an answer was given. A classification based on vocabulary might group different problems or bury a rare, expensive failure. The savings are easier to evaluate when each conclusion can be traced back to raw evidence.

A manager can spend the saved time interviewing more users. Another can commission a summary of old material and reduce direct contact. The two choices improve different internal metrics. Fieldwork hours, diversity of participation and product decisions that can be traced to evidence distinguish more research from a lower research budget.

### Illustrators

A client buying a single image to accompany an article has more substitutes than a producer who needs a consistent character in twenty scenes. A book commission can cover composition, revisions, narrative continuity and publication rights. Campaigns and games also need editable assets and the ability to reproduce poses, lighting and details under direction. Short commissions face an immediate substitution risk because the buyer has less accumulated knowledge to lose.

Hui, Reshef and Zhou also found short-run declines in work and income among freelancers exposed to text and image tools on a major platform. Illustrators with repeat clients and their own distribution may see a different trajectory. Their margins depend on bookings, licensing fees and how much of the week goes into unpaid bids and samples. [21]

### Commercial photographers

Catalogue photography includes capture, location or set, lighting, editing and adaptation to new formats. Generative backgrounds and automated retouching make some of that work cheaper. Generating the whole image is easier when the buyer wants a mood and can accept approximate representation. When colour, dimensions, finish or the condition of a particular item need to be established, faithful capture is part of the transaction.

Events, inspections and property listings include a documentary purpose. Inexpensive cameras and editing software changed these markets before generative AI. New methods alter the cost of editing and create additional kinds of visual supply. Labour data need to compare day rates, contracted image volume and editing time across services. An image bought to show that something happened has a different price structure from a generic visual.

### Video editors

Tools reduce the time needed to locate footage, caption, clean audio and create vertical versions. Well-organised source material can become cheaper to edit. Longer work requires point of view, timing, continuity and decisions about narrative priority. Consistency across videos requires approval and changes. The number of exports can rise faster than the hours for which an editor is paid.

A publisher previously buying one video a week could start posting to several channels. Where distribution depends on scarce attention, each new version competes against earlier output and competing publishers. Revenue per video, cost per approved minute and versions per contract show whether higher volume absorbs the labour saved.

### Animators

Storyboards, previsualisation and movement studies can be produced more quickly. The gain depends on whether the project is a prototype from a small studio or a production that must coordinate many scenes and contributors. A short sequence can absorb local corrections. Changes to design, placement or motion across a larger production can consume first-pass savings.

Entry jobs often include variations, cleanup and consistency work. Moving those tasks to a model can eliminate practice through which beginners learned production standards. Work can be reassigned to supervised blocking and review. Cost per approved scene, the share of shots returned and entry positions per production would distinguish how studios respond.

### Voice actors and narrators

Synthetic speech can compete for utilitarian narration and provide language coverage for projects that would otherwise have no audio. Advertisements, games and character performances include direction, responses to context and usage rights. Digital replicas make later uses possible without a fresh session. Consent, license scope and duration, and future compensation determine how much of the re-use value reaches the performer. SAG-AFTRA agreements for video games and for film and television give concrete examples of such issues in collective bargaining. [22]

The fee for an initial recording can remain stable even as the number of sessions per campaign falls. A larger one-off fee can coexist with lower cumulative income. The relevant transaction includes all paid uses and the performer's ability to refuse additional types of use. Monthly employment statistics capture little of this contract change.

## Writing, translation, journalism and marketing

### Advertising copywriters

Product descriptions, campaign email and ad variations have relatively well-defined deliverables. In a trial involving 453 college-educated professionals, Noy and Zhang found 40% less time spent and 18% higher quality on bounded writing tasks with ChatGPT. A trial can measure the quality of an individual submission. An agency also decides how many versions to buy, how to verify claims and what to budget for brand approval. One commission can turn into a purchase of several drafts and one approved version. [23]

Pressure may appear in contractor spending before it reaches the advertiser's payroll. Fixed-price writers can retain the gain until the agreement is revised. If variants become part of the same fee, output can rise without a proportional gain for the writer. Changes in sales attributable to the text and net earnings per hour provide more information than word counts.

### Technical writers

An API, machine or internal policy behaves in a particular way that its documentation has to describe correctly. AI can produce a structure, examples and a list of possible omissions. A draft without access to the product's actual operating rules can look complete while carrying errors. Bad instructions create costs for support, engineers and readers.

Employers previously unable to justify comprehensive documentation can begin to publish guides. The future of the role depends on who checks examples, tracks version changes and receives user feedback. The rate of updates, coverage of actual behaviour and readership indicate whether the service expanded or was absorbed into other jobs.

### Translators

Machine translation had already changed the occupation. Generative models can adapt tone, handle longer material and reuse context. A full translation can become a post-editing contract at a lower price per word, with responsibility for the final version still assigned to a human. The arithmetic depends on speed and verification. A thousand-word document paying 100 and taking five hours yields 20 an hour before expenses. Post-editing at 55 yields about 18.33 if it takes three hours and 27.50 if it takes two. The price per word falls in both cases while hourly earnings move in different directions.

Teutloff and colleagues estimated declines of 20% to 50% relative to the counterfactual trend in substitutable skill clusters such as writing and translation, with especially strong pressure on short projects. Technical and literary markets add requirements for terminology, cultural fit, confidentiality and consistency across long texts. The occupation's income distribution can shift even if translators with specialist clients retain those relationships. [6, 24]

### Interpreters

An interpreter supplies understanding at the moment people need to communicate. Translation captions may cover routine meetings whose organisers would never have paid for an interpreter. Hearings, negotiations and conversations with overlapping speakers require timely intervention and clarification. Cheaper technology can increase the number of multilingual conversations and displace different shares of paid work in each setting. US official projections aggregate translators and interpreters, leaving written and real-time services inseparable in that headline series. [25]

### Journalists

AI changes production costs and the route by which reporting reaches the reader. Transcribing, sorting and adapting material can take fewer hours. Gaining access to documents, interviewing sources, verifying statements and taking responsibility for publication continue to require time and trust. Meanwhile, a search engine that answers a query directly can reduce visits to the organisation that produced the original information.

Pew observed fewer clicks on search results when an AI summary appeared in a sample of browsing by US adults. Differences in the mix of queries may contribute to the result. The Reuters Institute reported a contraction in Google organic referrals for a sample of more than 2,500 sites using Chartbeat data, alongside other changes in news distribution. A publisher can pay less to process material and earn less with which to finance it. Revenue by channel, subscriptions and investment in original reporting are needed to determine the employment result. [26, 27]

### Marketing analysts

Generative tools can produce ads and descriptions and make it easier for customers to find products. Field experiments in online retail found sales gains varying from zero to 16.3% across workflows, depending on what an operation already provided. A conversion gain establishes value in that process. Competition, advertising prices and the firm's ability to interpret outcomes determine how much of it finances additional hiring. [28]

Cheap variants increase the return to a process that can run controlled experiments. As all advertisers gain the ability to produce more creative, distribution and attention can become binding. A provider paid for generic production faces different pressure from a team able to measure incremental sales and manage spending. Access to data and the ability to attribute an effect can change the distribution of pay.


## Customer service, outbound sales, data entry and administration

### Customer support representatives

In a staggered rollout of an AI assistant covering 5,172 support agents, Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond found 15% more problems resolved per hour. Effects differed across agents, with larger gains among less experienced workers and workers who started with lower productivity. Some of the improvement came from shorter conversations and greater ability to handle tickets. The tool spread guidance already present in the organisation. It could make a worker more capable during training and lower the time required to handle a fixed volume. [29]

Autonomous customer service changes the operating model. When the system deals with predictable requests, humans receive cases with more exceptions, disappointed customers or decisions requiring authorisation. A high containment rate can conceal repeat contacts and time shifted to the customer. Cost per durably resolved case, reopened issues and supervisory work belong in the comparison. Outsourced contracts paid by the seat or minute transmit the same productivity gain differently from changes to an internal team.

### Outbound contact-centre sales workers

Scripted selling begins with selection of contacts and an attempt to obtain a response. AI can lower the cost of tailoring an approach, send more messages and summarise calls. As more suppliers do the same, buyers can become saturated and each additional message can lose effectiveness. Consent, channel reputation and handoffs to human salespeople may matter more than the capacity to contact people at scale. Cost per contact offers little reassurance if cost per completed sale net of cancellations rises.

Automated qualification can concentrate commissions among workers responsible for closing sales. Employment analysis should separate workers who prepare lists, initiate conversations and negotiate. The ILO includes contact-centre salespeople in the highest exposure group. That gives a reason to examine hiring and shifts across the different stages of the sale. [12]

### Data-entry clerks

Document recognition and system integration were already replacing manual form entry. Models broaden the range of irregular documents from which fields can be extracted. A simple task disappears when information travels from its source to its destination without manual input. The remaining staff may work on exceptions, duplicated records and errors that have propagated across systems.

The ILO places data-entry clerks in the highest exposure group. BLS projects a 25.5% decline in US data-entry employment from 2025 to 2035, following decades of digitisation. Operational measures should include correct straight-through processing and hours needed to repair exceptions. Labour measures should include hiring and pay at entry and in supervisory roles. [12, 30]

### Administrative assistants and secretaries

Calendars, minutes, purchase requests and draft correspondence can draw from digital records. Automation has more scope where responsibilities and rules are documented. In smaller organisations, an assistant also knows who to call about a missing document, which problems are urgent and how to resolve conflicts between departments. Digitising some duties can help a person cover more offices. It can also allow firms to divide duties among managers and leave a vacant role unfilled.

The employment relationship may look stable until a departure. Headcount, voluntary exits and new hires measure distinct movements. Comparisons by workplace, size and mix of responsibilities keep executive support and medical office work visible within administrative statistics.

### Payroll and personnel administration clerks

Enterprise systems already automate calculations governed by well-established rules. AI can interpret documents, prepare replies, categorise requests and look for discrepancies at scale. Employers may need fewer people checking repetitive calculations while placing more weight on verifying that employee records support the resulting payment and paperwork.

An exception can produce reprocessing, an incorrect payment and time spent by workers trying to correct it. A firm can report higher throughput while handling more complaints. Employees processed per operator, time to correct exceptions and hiring in payroll occupations connect operational cost to labour demand. Payroll clerks are in the ILO's highest exposure group. [12, 30]

### Recruiters

Drafting vacancies, scheduling interviews and screening application documents are scalable tasks. Candidates can also automate the tailoring of application materials, increasing the volume of well-presented submissions. Employers may respond with more automated filtering and formal requirements. Time per application may fall while underlying competence becomes harder to identify.

The effect on recruiters depends on what the firm does with the saved time. Structured interviews, practical evaluation and communication with candidates need attention. A target based on processing more documents with fewer people can make mistaken exclusions harder to contest. Interview and hiring rates by recruitment channel, experience and qualifications show more than the count of applications processed.

## Accounting, banking, insurance, law and consulting

### Bookkeeping and accounting clerks

Sorting invoices, entering records and reconciling accounts consumes time at scale. A field study published in the *Journal of Accounting Research* links generative AI use to less routine data entry and more attention to customer communication and quality assurance. It combines operational data and practitioner surveys. Associations between intensity of use and outcomes can also reflect differences between firms and users. The reported allocation of tasks is observable within the study sample. [31]

A fixed group of clients can require fewer bookkeeping hours. A practice whose growth was limited by data entry may use the capacity to add clients. Firms offering only standardised compliance work are more exposed to lower pricing when rivals automate. For clerks, the outcome depends on whether jobs evolve towards reconciliation and client service or whether the practice starts recruiting people with more experience directly.

### Accountants and auditors

Automated entries and documentation can accelerate closing. Accounting and audit work also involve classifying ambiguous events, evaluating controls and taking responsibility for information supplied to others. An incorrect conclusion can affect creditors, investors and clients. The full cost includes reproducing the analysis and explaining why an exception was accepted.

Quickly prepared working papers can create room to review more transactions. They can also create more interpretations for a supervisor to validate. A firm paid by project has an incentive to limit hours. A firm whose reputation relies on strong controls has a reason to use some of the savings on broader examination. Hours by risk category, subsequent corrections and senior review time reveal the difference. [31]

### Financial and investment analysts

Collecting statements, copying tables and preparing comparable reports are time-consuming but often follow recognisable procedures. Models can draft reports quickly and allow analysts to follow more firms. A useful analysis must also determine when a series has changed definition, whether an acquisition changed consolidation and whether income came from a non-recurring item. A polished forecast can propagate a mistaken assumption across many decisions.

Broader coverage can absorb time savings. Fewer analysts can produce the same coverage when customers value a fixed volume. Entry positions may involve less mechanical work and more verification of assumptions. The industry then needs a way for skills once learned by constructing a model to be developed through assisted validation as well. Financial analysts are among the occupations in the highest ILO exposure group. [12]

### Credit analysts

Receiving documents and extracting information precede a lending decision. In standard applications, AI can assemble files and send cases through established rules. Irregular income, ambiguous purposes and missing data still require somebody to resolve discrepancies. The economic gain depends on processing cost and mistakes introduced or avoided.

A lender can profitably examine applications formerly too expensive to process, which could expand credit and work on exceptions. A different lender can maintain lending volume and reduce back-office staffing. Decision time, later default experience, review of ambiguous cases and staffing composition provide complementary evidence. [12]

### Insurance claims analysts

An insurer manages documents, messages, coverage and assessments of loss. Automated triage can route simple cases more quickly. Contested cases require interpretation, evidence and an explanation for an adverse decision. A faster distribution of work can improve service. Premature closure creates reopened cases, grievances and review.

Staff handling a larger proportion of complex cases can appear less productive. Comparing claims by severity and policy type helps distinguish changes in case mix from changes in performance. Demand for specialist analysts may grow while demand for routine forms processing falls. [30]

### Bank tellers

Demand for branch transactions already changed with cash machines, online banking, apps and instant payments. Brazil's central bank publishes Pix data, and Febraban's banking technology survey tracks financial institutions and channels. AI can make spoken or written queries easier to handle digitally, including some questions still directed to a counter. Attributing the entire subsequent movement in employment to the newest tool would overlook the systems deployed over previous years. [32]

Branches can also move towards renegotiation, sales and assistance for people with specific needs. Transaction work can shrink even while the bank continues hiring in the region. Hours, sales targets, distance to branches and the availability of accessible channels complete the assessment for workers and customers.

### Paralegals and legal assistants

Finding cases, locating clauses and organising records suit first-pass automation. An apparently complete output can omit a relevant authority or include a source that does not exist. Evaluations of legal research products have documented errors even when tools used search over specialist material. Source verification remains part of the service. [33]

Cheaper routine research can affect entry hiring of assistants and junior associates. Review can become a training assignment when staff compare evidence, discuss alternatives and receive feedback. Progression and independently demonstrated ability after a period of practice are more informative measures of skill formation than files processed.

### Lawyers

Reasoning models and integrated retrieval have increased productivity on specified legal tasks in recent experiments. The service also involves choosing a strategy, dealing with the client, negotiating and taking responsibility for advice. Routine matters can become more accessible and put pressure on hourly billing. Faster preparation might allow more alternatives to be explored in difficult matters. [34]

Fee structures change the labour incentives. A firm paid for a straightforward result can raise its margin or lower prices. A firm billing by time must find a different way to sustain revenue. Both need to price review, traceability and professional responsibility. The full effect on quality includes mistakes and their correction, as well as the time saved producing a first draft.

### Management consultants

Market research and a first slide deck involve organising information, writing and comparing options. In the trial with 758 Boston Consulting Group consultants, AI sped up tasks within the model's capabilities. On a task requiring reconciliation of evidence that the model handled poorly, AI users were 19 percentage points less likely to reach the right answer. A single average across apparently similar assignments conceals these differences. [35]

A client is also buying access to an operation, communication and accountability for advice. A supplier that sells a standard document faces more exposure to in-house production by the buyer. A supplier that changes what it sells can add implementation, follow-up or knowledge transfer. Employment outcomes depend on how projects per team and analysts per project change and on whether new assignments generate roles open to recent graduates.

## Healthcare, education, manufacturing and logistics

### Medical transcriptionists

Speech recognition and automated notes reduce typing. The work that remains includes patient identification, terminology, medication details and correction in context. When clinicians edit generated notes themselves, work moves into another occupation. The transcription provider may lose revenue while the clinical team absorbs verification. A measure of savings needs to include minutes worked on both sides. [12, 13]

A clinic can use the tool to improve records and spend more time in consultation. Another can increase visits per shift without reducing a clinician's net time spent documenting cases. Relevant measures include revision time, subsequent corrections and the responsibility assigned to clinical staff. Price per audio minute only tells us about the market for outsourced transcription.

### Radiologists

Sweden's MASAI trial evaluates a diagnostic system and a specified clinical workflow. Screen-reading volume in mammography was about 44% lower in the AI-supported group under the tested protocol. Later results found a non-inferior interval-cancer rate and higher sensitivity. The estimates come from a defined screening programme with clinical criteria and follow-up. [36]

A system with waiting lists can use freed capacity to expand screening or move workers to other imaging services. One facing fixed demand can buy fewer reading hours. Either trajectory is compatible with the clinical result. Waiting times, coverage, hours purchased and quality by patient population are needed before translating reduced reading volume into employment.

### Teachers

Lesson plans, adapted exercises and feedback can be drafted more quickly. In a classroom, teachers also identify the source of a difficulty, manage interaction and guide assessment. Abundant exercises add little when the institution cannot tell which concept a student has mastered. AI can make individual follow-up easier where staff can verify what students know and spend more time on difficult cases.

Under a fixed budget, administrators can add activities or reduce support. Preparation hours, workload per class, unaided performance, attendance and continuity of learning help distinguish these choices. Time saved supplying answers and time spent developing mastery have different relationships to the goal of education.

### Tutors

In the Tutor CoPilot trial, students matched with AI-supported tutors were four percentage points more likely to master the mathematical topics being taught. The system supported the human teacher in real time. The result depends on the instructional design and form of interaction tested. Institutions applying the same principle to entry hiring and coaching may be able to expand coverage without requiring extensive experience of each new tutor. [37]

Expansion also depends on funding, infrastructure, participation and the students available. Better sessions can create a service in communities that lacked tutoring. In a contract aimed at cutting the cost of serving a fixed number of students, the mix of experienced and inexperienced tutors can change. Learning, waiting times, persistence and earnings belong in the evaluation.

### Industrial operators

Industrial robots, computer vision and process control follow a different adoption path from chatbots. Deployment needs equipment, changes to the plant, maintenance and often integration with physical safety measures. A supplier can demonstrate a higher rate at a single station. Management also needs to show that the rest of the line can handle it and that downtime, defects and maintenance have been counted.

Acemoglu and Restrepo document adverse local employment and wage effects of higher robot exposure in the United States during their study period. Dauth and colleagues find displacement of manufacturing jobs in Germany with an offset in services. New jobs elsewhere can preserve a region's aggregate balance while displaced workers must change location, accept different wages or acquire other qualifications. These cases show how institutions and economic geography can alter the outcome of comparable technologies. [38–40]

### Warehouse and inventory workers

Physical automation relies on warehouse layout, predictable products and enough volume to recoup equipment costs. Vision and inventory systems can reduce repetitive checks. Picking heterogeneous orders, handling damage and processing returns require exceptions. An operator may monitor more lines or receive work only when the system cannot finish an order.

Seasonal demand and promised delivery times affect the automation decision. Firms can expand volume, hire fewer temporary workers or set more demanding targets. Hours and accidents per unit handled, seasonal hiring and turnover show how productivity and work intensity change. Robot installation totals describe the equipment stock. Labour outcomes have to be measured inside the operation. [40]

## The United States, Denmark and Brazil measure different transitions

In the ADP payroll sample studied by the Stanford Digital Economy Lab, employment among US workers aged 22 to 25 in exposed occupations was 19% below where it would have been had it followed the path of less-exposed peers through June 2026. Hiring accounted for most of the gap. The result uses a comparative benchmark. It is different from a share of workers dismissed or a fall in national employment. Controls for education reduce the difference. Some trends predate generative AI, and the ADP sample shows a larger separation than national surveys do. [3]

A Census Bureau working paper using Quarterly Workforce Indicators also finds weaker early-career labour demand among 22- to 24-year-olds in highly exposed industry-state cells. Adjusted employment fell by about 12% in the highest exposure quintile in the ten quarters after ChatGPT's public release, while it stayed stable among less-exposed cells. The paper finds signs of earlier trend movements and attributes some of the relative decline to monetary policy shocks in its historical decomposition. Grouping by industry and state captures different patterns from grouping payroll records by occupation. Partial agreement locates some of the weakness in hiring without treating a product launch as an isolated experiment. [4]

Chandar and Klein Teeselink study foreign affiliates in 41 countries. The junior share falls relative to controls after adoption, mainly because senior employment grows. A team that goes from 20 senior and 10 junior workers to 30 senior and 10 junior workers shifts its junior share from one third to one quarter without losing any junior seats. If production triples without increasing that group, entry opportunities per unit of output fall. Counts, shares and rates measured against an appropriate denominator should all be observed. [41]

Denmark offers a view of other outcomes. Linked records indicate changes in tasks and rule out effects larger than 2% on the studied earnings and hours outcomes in the first two years. Training, contracts and work organisation can absorb some disruption without an immediate drop in earnings. These estimates discourage extrapolation from young US hires and invite follow-up as firms integrate the tools more deeply and affected workers subsequently look for new employment. [7]

In Brazil, TIC Empresas found that adoption of some form of AI rose from 13% in 2024 to 17% in 2025. Adoption among firms with 250 or more people employed rose from 38% to 50%. In the smallest eligible group of 10 to 49 people employed it rose from 10% to 15%. The study covers firms above its stated threshold and includes several types of AI. The size difference shows how competitive pressure can arrive before a firm adopts directly. A small design studio may lose an order when a large client brings work in-house even if the studio never purchased the tools. [42]

FGV IBRE researchers estimate that 29.6% of workers in the third quarter of 2025 were in occupations with some exposure to generative AI. About 5.2 million workers were in the highest gradient. The calculation uses occupational composition and the ILO task index. An exposed worker can be employed at a firm that has not adopted AI. A firm that has adopted it can employ people whose tasks have barely changed. Crossing those proportions with incompatible denominators would yield a misleading displacement measure. [43]

Using Novo Caged, Scintilla found that 18- to 24-year-olds accounted for 35.2% of formal hires in its broad high-exposure group in 2022 and 35.0% in 2025. In administrative work, the share moved from 43.2% to 42.2%. Separately, Daniel Duque found indications of adverse changes in employment and earnings for 18- to 29-year-olds in more exposed groups, using PNAD Contínua and exposure predicted from characteristics predating AI diffusion. The first indicator measures the composition of registered hires. The second studies whether people are employed and what they earn. A stable share of new hires is compatible with worse prospects for some of the people looking for work. [43, 44]

### Where Brazilian effects may appear first

Brazil combines large companies able to integrate tools and small suppliers that sell work as it is needed. A bank may reduce time spent checking documents and widen lending to small firms. It can also renegotiate volume and price with an outsourced contact centre. The national adoption average cannot distinguish the two decisions. Evidence needs to locate where the saving occurs and which agreement gets repriced first.

Exposure has a geography. Digital work sold to buyers in another region or country can fall in price even when demand in the worker's city has not changed. Local administration and customer service can depend on units in larger markets. Production elsewhere and profits at a foreign platform can support consumption while the local income of a supplier falls. This differs from a factory closure, but workers still need access to different buyers and employers.

Novo Caged records monthly formal hires and separations. RAIS records annual employment stocks and characteristics. The Labour Ministry had released the September 2026 Novo Caged data by the evidence cutoff. National formal employment totals provide business-cycle context. They leave AI's contribution in individual occupations unidentified. PNAD Contínua extends observation to employees, employers, own-account workers and informal employment. The household survey's July–September 2026 quarterly release was scheduled for 18 November. [45–47]

The CBO classification used in administrative records, the household survey coding and ISCO-08 differ. Product design, graphic design and user research may be coded near one another while involving distinct work. Many-to-many mappings and small sample sizes require disclosure of the level of aggregation and uncertainty. The pre-2020 employment series needs to reflect the change in Caged methodology. Age, advertised seniority and an observed first registered job need to be distinguished when the data permit. [12, 45, 47]

Within each occupation, the most informative comparison would follow youth hiring as a share of the previous employment stock, the gap with experienced workers and real pay among those hired. If the youth share remains stable because all hiring fell, volumes reveal the deterioration. If median pay rises because low-paid jobs disappear, changes in the composition of successful hires explain part of the gain. Comparison across similar workplaces is stronger when deployment can be dated and trends observed beforehand. Exposure alone supports evidence of association. The Brazilian occupation-specific findings here come from the cited studies and public releases. An integrated series linking realised adoption to employment links and freelance earnings across occupations remains unavailable in those sources. [3, 4, 43–47]

## Why deployment can outrun retraining

### An employer changes a purchase. A worker rebuilds a career.

A firm can stop buying weekly contractor reports and move some work to a new tool within a month. The contractor must find new buyers or establish proficiency in a different role. The first choice occurs inside one organisation. The second involves education, job search, financial support and recognition by an employer. When recruiting requires experience that comes only from the first job, independent study addresses part of the barrier.

Historical optimism points to new occupations created after technological change. New jobs are compatible with lasting costs for particular people. Autor, Dorn and Hanson's study of US import competition analyses a different shock. It documents how national outcomes can coexist with persistent adverse effects on jobs and wages in places hit unevenly. A programmer who moves into technical sales needs experience that buyers recognise. A designer who moves city faces housing and commuting costs. Expected future earnings do not automatically cover current living expenses. [48]

Education has its own timing. A course can teach the mechanics of using a tool within weeks. Checking conclusions, diagnosing failures and explaining decisions to customers require practice and feedback. When employers withdraw assignments through which newcomers learned, training programmes need to supply other ways to practise. Evaluation should show whether graduates handle unfamiliar problems and whether the resulting skills lead to a better job.

### Stable employment can conceal a shift in risk

Firms accumulate knowledge inside established teams. While demand and model quality are uncertain, they can leave vacancies open, reduce outsourcing or rely on temporary hiring before touching core staff. A stable unemployment rate is compatible with those adjustments. The major change may show up in the probability that a worker who loses a job can return to work at comparable pay.

Platforms reveal part of this mechanism as contracts turn over quickly. A November 2026 study by Upwork and OpenAI found that AI-related listings were associated with larger fixed-price project budgets and more hours, while posted hourly rates were similar. A pricier project may simply contain more work. Combinations of tasks reflect several changes, including automation, integration and novel requests. The data come from firms with direct interests in this market. Independent studies of falling demand in other categories show how gains and losses can occur in the same kind of marketplace. [5, 6, 21, 49]

### Distribution of the savings

An hour saved can finance a higher margin, a lower price, higher wages, a shorter working week or greater production. Its destination depends on competition for customers, the price of models and cloud services, access to distribution and bargaining. Where buyers are concentrated, a freelancer can face pressure to cut prices before finding new clients. Dube and colleagues measured monopsony power in a different on-demand platform before the spread of generative AI. The result leaves the magnitude in design or software open while motivating attention to the worker's ability to switch buyers. [50]

Where demand goes unmet, the social gain can exceed what appears in profit margins. A clinic can reduce waiting times. Less expensive legal assistance can reach families who went without it. Expansion depends on financing and other inputs. With fixed budgets, unmet demand can persist even when an individual task becomes cheaper. Investment in training, redesigned processes and intangible assets helps explain the lag between tool availability and aggregate productivity. [36, 51]

## The cost of reorganising income, time and careers

### Recalculating labour demand from accepted outputs

Two extensions make the hours calculation more realistic. The first includes verification. Suppose an office took eight hours to produce a deliverable and two to verify it. AI halves production time. With unchanged review time the output takes six hours, a 40% saving. If review rises to four hours because the documents are more numerous or inconsistent, the output takes eight hours and the saving is 20%. The second extension concerns volume. At eight hours per output, 125 completed items absorb the same thousand hours as a hundred did originally. Identical claims about first-draft speed can support very different staffing decisions.

| Illustrative scenario | Production per unit | Review per unit | Accepted outputs | Total hours |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Initial operation | 8 hours | 2 hours | 100 | 1,000 |
| AI with stable review time and fixed demand | 4 hours | 2 hours | 100 | 600 |
| AI with twice the review time and fixed demand | 4 hours | 4 hours | 100 | 800 |
| AI with twice the review time and more demand | 4 hours | 4 hours | 125 | 1,000 |

The example assumes comparable output quality. For employees, demand elasticity and budgets partly determine the response. For contractors, commission frequency, market competition and pricing power determine how much of the gain remains in earnings. From the customer's perspective, review after delivery belongs in the cost. When suppliers transfer verification to buyers, the process can look cheaper to the supplier while consuming more time in total.

### Re-employment and the cost of waiting

A worker who earned 100 per month, loses income for five months and returns at 90 loses 570 units in the first year, before transfers and search expenses. Re-employment ends the spell without work but leaves part of the wage loss. If savings funded the wait, household wealth takes longer to recover. This illustrative calculation explains why counting a lost job and a newly created one gives an incomplete picture of welfare. Housing, caring obligations and commuting can make access to a vacancy in a different region too expensive.

Recent graduates and older workers face different barriers. The former need recognised experience and may have had little time to save or make insurance contributions. The latter can have substantial knowledge of a particular workflow and face lower wages when they transfer it to another role. Age, experience and education capture different mechanisms. Useful outcomes include time to find new work, real earnings after six or twelve months, and tenure in the next job. For own-account workers, recovery also means rebuilding a repeat customer base and comparable net earnings over several months.

### Similar exposure can produce different kinds of inequality

The ILO estimates that its highest-exposure gradient accounts for 4.7% of women's global employment and 2.4% of men's. Differences in occupational composition, including administrative work, contribute to the gap. These estimates concern exposure. Workers with the same title can have different responsibilities and unequal access to training, connectivity, equipment and professional referrals. [12, 43, 44]

The speed of adoption at a firm and a household's capacity to absorb lost income are separate measures. A worker paying for care, requiring a particular schedule or financing their own training can face a different cost of adjustment. Analysis by race and gender needs sufficiently large samples, attention to occupation and geography, and transparency about hiring channels. An average across exposed occupations can conceal what happened to groups underrepresented in the expanding roles.

### Public administration and the ability to obtain a decision

Benefits officers and administrative intake workers process requests under specified rules and with prescribed records. An assistant can guide applicants and route correctly documented claims earlier. The social effect also depends on who falls through. People with incomplete records, exceptional cases or unreliable digital access may spend more time obtaining help. A saving in the agency budget can reappear as time spent by the citizen.

Where headcount is constrained, faster processing can reduce waiting times and free staff for harder cases. A target based on response times or resolved cases can reward premature closure. Time to final decision, successful appeals, access to human support and regional differences give a richer account. Public employment responds to hiring rules, civil service careers and budgets. Contractors can lose positions more quickly than permanent staff. [9]

### Retail cashiers and shop-floor work

Self-checkout, electronic payments and computer vision change physical flows, queues and staffing. An attendant can cover several checkout stations. The remaining work includes payment problems, price discrepancies, restricted items and help for customers. Savings depend on the number of stations staff can supervise while keeping losses and mistakes under control.

Scanning, payment and receipt generation have a different technological history from language models. Recent AI can affect customer service, inventory and the handling of exceptions, each of which needs its own evidence. Shrinkage, peak staffing hours, wait times and turnover complement output measures. As in warehouses, location, size and store layout have a greater effect on physical deployment than a worker's access to an online subscription. [30, 40]

### Measuring a recovery when job requirements change

A vacancy labelled junior can require autonomy once expected of someone at mid-career. Another can drop the word while remaining open to a beginner. Classifying openings requires reading the experience requirements, autonomy, qualifications and contract type. Methods should account for differences by sector, city and platform. Deduplication by text and employer matters when one employer advertises the same job in several places, because listing distribution is easy to confuse with job growth.

Pay can conceal selection too. When firms hire fewer workers and concentrate hiring on more experienced people, observed earnings can rise as opportunities for the typical beginner worsen. Comparing listed and contracted wages, medians and lower deciles, and applications and hires helps identify this mechanism. A convincing recovery in access would raise the number of opportunities, maintain accessible requirements or supply explicit training, and support real earnings among workers with comparable experience.

### Tools, training time and access to income

Learning to use a tool and moving into a new occupation involve several kinds of learning. Instrumental learning makes it easier to query a database or obtain an explanation. Work-specific learning involves understanding a firm's data, identifying the source of an audit discrepancy or explaining the behaviour of a production system. Recognition is a separate stage. Portfolios, supervised work and references allow others to trust a person's competence. AI can speed up instrumental learning and create opportunities for work-specific practice. If placements disappear, gaining credibility can become harder just as courses become easier to obtain.

An individual course works best for someone who already has time, income, equipment and a place to apply the material. Training inside a job gives workers an operating setting and requires supervision. Its future benefit can go to the trainee, the current employer and future employers. Sector-wide arrangements can share some of the cost when firms are too small to run programmes alone. Evaluation should consider whether participants obtain work elsewhere, which distinguishes portable skills from reliance on one employer.

Some control over the timing of change has economic value for workers. Advance notice of a contract change gives a provider more time to redirect their search and build a financial buffer. Income during the transition affects which courses, travel and jobs are feasible. When contracts are renewed month to month, the disappearing commission can be the first sign of a change. A protection system responding only to formal dismissals addresses part of the shock. Income, employment and platform demand may reveal pressure before unemployment records move.

### Five responses and the outcomes that can test them

**Paid, supervised practice.** A firm can employ beginners and alternate execution with supervised verification of AI outputs. Supervision has an explicit cost. Learning should be tested on unfamiliar work. Tenure, unaided performance and promotion rates indicate whether the arrangement works as a path into a career. Enrolment measures programme reach. Comparing outcomes with similar candidates provides evidence on whether the programme widened access. [19, 52]

**Employment services and demand-linked retraining.** A programme that targets adjacent task families can reduce the cost of changing roles. A technical translator might apply specialist terminology to documentation review. An experienced data-entry worker could move into records quality. Both possibilities depend on actual vacancies and pay. Assessment should include time to find work, the share of earnings recovered and outcomes among comparable people without support. Candidates need to know whether there are relevant local jobs, remote options or a requirement to move.

**Income support during transitions.** Unemployment insurance and temporary wage-loss support operate through different channels. The former maintains some income during search. Temporary support for someone accepting a lower-paid job may speed return to work when the old position is no longer available. An arrangement that subsidises persistently low-wage work without progression may undermine job quality. Track tenure, earnings as the support ends and subsequent progression. Eligibility for independent workers is harder to determine, since a fall in revenue and the end of a business seldom coincide in one observable event.

**Sharing time and productivity gains.** A shorter working week at unchanged pay is one use of productivity when the same quality and volume require fewer hours. Firms can also share results through pay or reserve paid time for training. A contact centre with an unserved queue can use savings to expand service instead. In other sectors, higher targets can intensify work even when individual tasks take less time. Actual hours, absence, turnover, customer satisfaction and changes in pay help explain who benefited. [29, 51]

**Purchasing and contractor terms.** In translation, design and software, clients can pay less for a draft while expecting the same guarantees for the final product. Itemising production, review, rights and maintenance shows where costs persist. Public procurement needs to establish who serves citizens when an automated process fails and who is responsible for mistakes. Creative work can earn a different lifetime return when usage terms change despite an unchanged upfront fee. Voice agreements show why the terms for future re-use affect current negotiations. [22, 33]

### Evidence that could change the diagnosis

A sustained increase in hiring of inexperienced workers in exposed professions would weaken the argument that entry has narrowed. The case would be stronger if headcounts, rates relative to employment, comparable pay and learning outcomes improved together. A rebound confined to applicants with several years of experience would show a shift in selection. In freelancing, sustained growth in net hourly earnings and stable access for new workers would be more informative than larger listed budgets on projects requiring more hours. [1–6, 41, 49]

There is a separate employer-side test. New verification, complex service and integration roles can offset some task substitution if organisations hire enough people to do them. The offset needs to be measured over time across compatible occupations and places. Demand for complementary work can grow while some of the people displaced take longer to reach it. That lag explains why a firm-level assessment and the experience of a specific cohort can tell different stories.

## Three paths from 2027 to 2030

### Rising demand and renewed entry routes

Lower costs make previously shelved projects viable. Smaller businesses buy automation and services once beyond their budgets. Teams invest part of the saving in testing, adjustment and training. Beginners gain supervised positions and gradually demonstrate that they can work independently. The pattern would appear in rising finished output together with more entry hiring in absolute terms, better career progression and stable or rising real starting pay.

Weak usage despite higher production, slower promotion and fewer replacement internships would count against that scenario. Growth in specialist jobs requiring previous experience offers limited evidence of a widely accessible transition. Complementary tasks have to become paid work that people affected elsewhere can reach.

### Concentration of work in experienced teams

Employers retain managers and specialists and buy less routine execution. Employment moves little at first, with adjustment happening through replacement hiring and spending on contractors. Incumbents produce more and take on more concentrated verification duties. They may receive part of the benefit. Job search takes longer for beginners. Contractors in exposed markets accept lower rates, increase volume or find something else to sell.

Sustained growth in junior hiring in the same exposed specialities, recovery in hourly earnings from paid contracts and more internal routes to mid-career positions would weaken this trajectory. Productivity can give a firm a short-run advantage. Lower investment in training can produce a shortage of qualified workers later.

### Uneven adaptation with institutional support

Sectors with measurable output quality and sufficient demand add complementary employment. Short-order activities retain pricing pressure and income risk. Collective agreements, supervised internships and re-employment policy can absorb losses for particular groups. Outcomes depend on who is covered and on the quality of the jobs that emerge. Training provided inside a job can make a tool part of an apprenticeship. Requiring independent training during unemployment shifts much of the cost to individuals.

Brazilian apprenticeships offer a precedent with a specific scope. Corseuil, Foguel and Gonzaga use linked records and an eligibility discontinuity from the early 2000s. Participants were more likely to obtain permanent jobs than comparable temporary workers, with outcomes varying across settings. The study predates generative AI. It supports investigation of paid practical training as a route into employment, assessed by labour market outcomes and tenure. Present-day evaluation would need to measure independent skills and earnings as well as enrolment. [52]

The paths differ in the time between losing an established opportunity and reaching a new one. If employers use savings to raise output, train beginners and share gains through pay, workers can adjust within the job. If old arrangements disappear before new routes open, the cost falls on people seeking a position or contract. Evidence through November 2026 is more consistent with rapid, concentrated change than with a simultaneous displacement across occupations. Total employment can recover before affected workers regain comparable earnings and careers.

## Sources and scope of the evidence

The evidence cutoff is 16 November 2026. Posting counts, completed tasks, demand on platforms, employment, pay, hours and occupational exposure have different denominators. The BLS matrix and design projections use the 2025–2035 edition. Occupational sections without direct employment studies describe conditional mechanisms. Field studies measure effects in the populations and workflows tested. Market-level hiring estimates depend on comparators and pre-existing trends. Age, occupational experience and job seniority describe different groups.
