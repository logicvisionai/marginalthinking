# Afghanistan: state formation, corridor dependence and the political economy of a landlocked society

Afghanistan's present system is shaped by difficult internal geography, repeated dependence on external resources, a narrow formal productive and financial base, and a state whose authority has historically been negotiated between central institutions and local structures. The Taliban's return to power in 2021 changed the governing order without removing those constraints. By 2026 the de facto authorities had consolidated nationwide territorial control and strengthened domestic revenue collection, while international recognition remained limited, formal finance remained shallow, aid was far below the republic era, and restrictions on women and girls were reducing human-capital formation.

This dossier complements **MT-ST-2026-09-28-afghanistan-post-2021**. The transition report asks what changed after 2021; this country context explains the deeper mechanisms through which that change operates.

**Information cutoff: 4 October 2026. Confidence: medium.** Afghanistan has no recent comprehensive census; official series are incomplete and estimates from the World Bank, UN agencies and de facto institutions are not always directly comparable.

## Geography is an economic institution

Afghanistan is landlocked between Iran, Pakistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan and China. Mountains fragment internal markets. Access to seaports depends on neighbors, so border closures transmit into freight costs, food availability, export income and customs revenue.

```map
title: Afghanistan's external-access system
Pakistan | historical access to Karachi and Port Qasim | politically exposed crossings
Iran | western trade and humanitarian access | diversification corridor
Uzbekistan/Turkmenistan | rail, fuel, electricity and trade | northern alternatives
Tajikistan/Kyrgyz Republic | CASA-1000 electricity linkage | regional power integration
China via Wakhan | direct border, limited mass-commercial infrastructure | strategic option
```

The 1893 Durand Line remains embedded in Afghanistan-Pakistan disputes and crosses social networks. Its present importance is practical: refugees, armed-group allegations, customs, transit and security remain intertwined.

## State formation combined coercion, bargaining and external finance

Modern state formation is commonly traced to the Durrani polity of 1747. The durable mechanism is repeated centralization of a diverse territory while bargaining with local, tribal, religious and regional authorities. Amir Abdur Rahman Khan's late-nineteenth-century consolidation expanded central reach with severe violence against resisting communities. Foreign aid later became an instrument of modernization and geopolitical balancing.

The Soviet intervention, anti-Soviet war, fall of the Najibullah government, civil war and first Taliban emirate transformed armed power and displaced millions. The 2001-2021 republic expanded education, health, communications and infrastructure but relied on exceptional external fiscal and security support. World Bank work estimated grants financed more than three quarters of total public expenditure near the end of the 2010s.

| Period | Settlement | Resource mechanism | Durable implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1747-1919 | dynastic state formation and imperial frontier | taxation, trade, tribute, external subsidy | center-periphery bargaining shapes state reach |
| 1919-1978 | independent monarchy, then republic | customs, agriculture, public investment, aid | modernization without eliminating regional inequality |
| 1978-1992 | PDPA/Soviet-backed state | large external military and fiscal support | capacity becomes sensitive to patron withdrawal |
| 1992-2001 | civil war, then first Taliban emirate | fragmented taxation, customs, patronage | monopoly of force and revenue incomplete |
| 2001-2021 | constitutional republic | domestic revenue plus large grants | services expand with external dependence |
| 2021-2026 | Taliban-led de facto order | taxes, customs, trade, externally delivered aid | stronger control with finance and human capital constrained |

## A smaller fiscal state

The republic's collapse removed much of the security payroll, development finance, technical assistance and normal financial connectivity. The replacement order operates with a smaller resource envelope. Before 2021, the World Bank estimated grants financed more than three quarters of total public expenditure, illustrating how deeply the fiscal system depended on external resources. The Spring 2026 update later estimated domestic revenue at 19.8% of GDP in 2025, reflecting stronger collection under a radically smaller state. This is evidence of administrative capacity, not service quality or political inclusion.

```flow
Territorial and border control → observable tax/customs base → domestic revenue → core administration
                         ↘ import dependence → customs exposure → border-disruption vulnerability
```

## Aggregate recovery is not household recovery

World Bank estimates changed materially as return migration accelerated, so vintages must be kept separate. The December 2025 update projected 2025 real GDP growth of 4.3%, population growth of 8.6% and a 4.0% decline in GDP per capita. The Spring 2026 Afghanistan Development Update later estimated 2025 real GDP growth at 4.8%, population growth around 11% and a 5.6% decline in GDP per capita. It also reported domestic revenue at 19.8% of GDP and a current-account deficit estimated at 36.1% of GDP. The September 2026 Economic Monitor again described per-capita income as under severe pressure and used a different FY2025 projection vintage. These series are not silently averaged here.

```chart
type: bar
title: Afghanistan: World Bank Spring 2026 estimate for 2025
unit: percent
Real GDP growth | 4.8
Population growth | 11.0
GDP per capita change | -5.6
Domestic revenue / GDP | 19.8
```

The revision history itself is analytically important. Aggregate output can grow while living standards fall when population expands faster than productive capital. The World Bank projected growth of about 4% for 2026, but emphasized that weak investment, border disruption, aid decline and regional instability can reduce that outcome.

Large return flows intensify the mechanism. UNHCR and IOM recorded about 1.082 million returns to Afghanistan in 2026 by 1 August, including roughly 654,100 from Pakistan and 426,700 from Iran. Returnees add demand and skills but also pressure jobs, housing, water, schools and health. Without corresponding capital formation, labor supply can grow faster than productive employment.

## Productive structure and trade

Agriculture remains a livelihood base and shock absorber. Irrigation, precipitation, groundwater and market access determine rural income; drought and floods transmit into food security, migration and debt. Industry is narrow. Construction and mining generate activity, but power, logistics, formal credit and long-horizon financing constrain industrial deepening.

Imports supply fuel, food, machinery and medicine; exports remain concentrated in agriculture, textiles, coal and selected minerals. Corridor diversification can make an import-dependent economy more resilient without making it more productive. Productivity requires firms able to scale competitive exports and domestic value added.

## Finance: price stability without full intermediation

The Afghani has been comparatively stable since the immediate post-2021 shock, but exchange-rate stability is not a normalized banking system. World Bank assessments continue to describe constrained lending, regulatory uncertainty, non-performing loans and cash outside formal banks.

Hawala networks move remittances and trade payments where correspondent banking is weak. They cannot fully substitute for transparent multi-year project finance, mortgages, scalable working-capital credit or a normal lender-of-last-resort architecture.

```diagram
Savings/remittances → cash + hawala → consumption and short-cycle trade
Formal banks → limited credit / compliance constraints → weak long-horizon investment
External capital → sanctions/compliance/recognition friction → high transaction cost
Result → money circulates while capital formation remains shallow
```

U.S. General License 20 permits a broad range of transactions involving Afghanistan subject to conditions; sanctions are not a blanket ban on commerce. Protected central-bank assets, correspondent-bank caution and compliance risk still raise transaction costs. Banking weakness is also domestic: World Bank monitoring has continued to flag limited lending and asset-quality problems, including non-performing loans. A stable exchange rate can therefore coexist with a system that does not allocate enough long-duration credit to productive firms.

## Women and girls are a productive-capacity constraint

Girls have been barred from formal schooling beyond grade six since September 2021 and women from universities since December 2022. UNAMA reported in March 2025 that 2.2 million girls had been affected by secondary-school exclusion. Restrictions also affect employment, mobility and public participation.

The mechanism is cumulative. Fewer girls completing secondary education means a smaller pipeline into teaching, medicine, administration and skilled private work. Suspension of women's medical education reduces future female health-worker supply where gender rules can make female staff necessary for women to receive care. That is not only a rights constraint; it is a direct transmission from education policy to health-system capacity, labour-force participation and household welfare. A 2026 UN Secretary-General report cited maternal mortality at 521 deaths per 100,000 live births.

UNAMA's March 2026 consultations found only 14% of women consulted reported access to formal dispute-resolution services versus 53% of men. This is perception-based evidence, not a national administrative statistic.

```flow
Education restrictions → smaller female professional pipeline → fewer teachers/health workers + lower household earnings → weaker services and human capital → lower potential productivity
```

The de facto authorities describe these policies through their interpretation of Islamic law and social norms. The observable institutional fact is continued exclusion; this dossier does not infer private motives.

## Demography, opium and minerals

The population is young, but the lack of a recent census makes precise levels uncertain. Mass returns are changing the denominator rapidly. A youth-heavy population becomes a demographic dividend only when education, health, capital and employment expand with it.

The Taliban's 2022 prohibition on opium poppy cultivation produced a large decline documented by UNODC, demonstrating enforcement capacity. It does not establish successful rural transformation: legal crops must replace income, credit and market access. Synthetic-drug trafficking is a separate risk and cannot be inferred from poppy acreage alone.

Afghanistan has documented copper, iron, chromite, talc, gemstones and other resources. Geological potential is not an economically recoverable reserve or fiscal asset. Commercial extraction requires drilling, metallurgy, power, water, transport, financing, legal enforceability and markets. Aynak illustrates the gap between a large deposit and operating industrial capacity.

## Water, electricity and regional bargaining

Afghanistan imports substantial electricity from neighbors. CASA-1000 can integrate the country into a Central Asia-South Asia electricity corridor, but planned transmission is not delivered power.

The Qosh Tepa Canal could expand northern irrigation while affecting Amu Darya water available downstream in Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan. Engineering, drainage, land allocation and actual withdrawals will determine both domestic gains and external effects.

## Security changed form

Nationwide conventional war declined sharply after 2021, but insecurity did not disappear. ISIL-K retains attack capacity, explosive remnants continue to harm civilians, and Afghanistan-Pakistan tensions create border and civilian risk. Pakistan states that Tehrik-e Taliban Pakistan operates from Afghan territory; Taliban authorities deny allowing Afghan territory to be used against other states. These are competing claims, not interchangeable facts.

Russia formally recognized the Taliban government in 2025; broader UN-level recognition has not followed. Engagement by China, Iran, Pakistan, Central Asian states, India and Gulf states varies by issue and is not equivalent to recognition. Pakistan remains central to transit and security but relations are burdened by border closures and competing claims over the Tehrik-e Taliban Pakistan: Pakistani authorities say the group operates from Afghan territory, while the Taliban authorities deny allowing Afghan territory to be used against other states. Iran is critical for trade, fuel and migration; Central Asia for electricity and northern logistics; China for trade and prospective mining; Gulf states for aviation, finance and mediation; and India for aid and commercial links. Geography therefore encourages multiple transactional relationships rather than a single external patron.

## External relations are more regional and transactional

The republic-era system was deeply tied to Western security and aid institutions. The post-2021 system relies more on neighboring trade, energy and diplomatic channels. Iran matters for trade and migration; Pakistan for transit, migration and security; Central Asia for electricity and logistics; China for potential mining and trade; Gulf states for aviation, finance and mediation; India for humanitarian and commercial engagement.

This is not a clean geopolitical pivot. Geography rewards multiple corridors rather than dependence on one patron.

## What would change the assessment

The assessment would improve with sustained real GDP-per-capita growth, deeper formal private credit, stable cross-border payments, restored girls' secondary and higher education, higher female employment, rising legal agricultural productivity, commercially operating large mines, reliable electricity expansion and export diversification.

It would weaken if return migration continues to outpace job creation, aid and services contract faster than domestic revenue can compensate, border conflict repeatedly interrupts trade, drought reduces rural incomes, or human-capital restrictions further shrink professional labor supply.

## Evidence limits and contrary evidence

Territorial consolidation and revenue gains contradict a simple failed-state description. Falling per-capita income, shallow finance and service constraints contradict a simple recovery description. Opium suppression demonstrates coercive reach but not a replacement rural economy. Currency stability does not prove financial normalization. Mineral resources do not constitute realized industrial capacity. Reduced conventional warfare does not establish inclusive institutions or eliminate terrorism, repression, explosive remnants or cross-border conflict.
