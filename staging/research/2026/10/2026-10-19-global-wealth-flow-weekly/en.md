# Global Wealth Flow Monitor — 19 October 2026

Foreign demand for long-term U.S. securities recovered sharply in August, but short-term dollar positions moved in the opposite direction. The U.S. Treasury recorded **$279.2 billion in adjusted net long-term securities inflows**, after **-$27.9 billion in July**. Foreign investors bought **$300.7 billion of U.S. long-term securities**, while U.S. residents purchased **$21.6 billion of foreign securities**. Foreign Treasury-bill holdings fell **$47.8 billion** and banks' own net dollar liabilities to foreign residents fell **$41.6 billion**. The result is a change in maturity composition, not a uniform movement of global wealth.

Newer fund data show a similar division. In the week ended 7 October, U.S. long-term funds and ETFs received **$12.35 billion combined**, with **$24.53 billion into bonds** but **$18.36 billion leaving domestic-equity funds**. U.S. money-market fund assets subsequently declined **$40.46 billion** in the week ended 14 October, remaining at **$7.923 trillion**. The populations and reporting periods differ: these figures cannot be added to the Treasury balance.

**Information cutoff: 19 October 2026, 00:20 Brasília time.** Treasury TIC refers to August, released 16 October; ICI long-term funds to 7 October, released 14 October; money-market funds to 14 October, released 15 October.

## Long-term inflows recovered while short-term dollar liabilities fell

The Treasury's 16 October release shows a **$307.1 billion** swing in adjusted net long-term securities transactions from July to August. Foreign purchases of U.S. long-term securities included **$257.8 billion by private investors** and **$42.9 billion by foreign official institutions**. Those two components are already part of the $300.7 billion foreign purchase total. The adjusted $279.2 billion balance also accounts for U.S. purchases abroad and technical adjustments; it is not an additional pool of money.

| Treasury TIC measure | July | August | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Adjusted net long-term transactions | -$27.9bn | +$279.2bn | Cross-border transaction flow |
| Foreign net purchases of U.S. long-term securities | +$40.6bn | +$300.7bn | Foreign portfolio demand |
| U.S. purchases of foreign securities | +$68.5bn | +$21.6bn | Outward acquisitions, positive purchase convention |
| Foreign change in Treasury-bill holdings | +$39.0bn | -$47.8bn | Short-term security position |
| Bank net dollar liabilities to foreign residents | +$50.1bn | -$41.6bn | Banking liability, not securities flow |
| Total net TIC inflow | +$87.4bn | +$199.8bn | Includes long/short securities and banking |

The series is volatile, not a monotonic recovery. The twelve-month net long-term total through August was **$1.425 trillion**, versus **$1.221 trillion** in the previous twelve months. Both are flows, not outstanding holdings. Foreign private purchases of Treasury notes and bonds reached **$90.9 billion** in August and official purchases **$18.1 billion**; these are subsets of the long-term total. Treasury warns that custodial reporting cannot reliably identify ultimate beneficial owners by country. TIC also excludes foreign direct investment, so it does not establish newly commissioned U.S. production capacity. [U.S. Treasury, 16 October](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0662).

## Bonds and world-equity funds attracted subscriptions despite domestic-equity redemptions

ICI's 14 October release revised the combined flow for the week ended 30 September to **-$2.299 billion**, compared with **-$1.969 billion** in the earlier vintage. The week ended 7 October then registered **+$12.351 billion**. Traditional long-term mutual funds lost **$35.87 billion** while ETFs issued **$48.22 billion** net. These are different investment wrappers; the figures do not prove that the same investors switched directly between them.

Domestic-equity funds lost **$18.355 billion**, world-equity funds gained **$6.949 billion**, and bond funds gained **$24.527 billion**. Within bonds, taxable funds received **$25.433 billion** and municipal funds lost **$0.906 billion**. The world-equity category does not identify the final countries receiving investment. Nor does the bond total reveal duration, credit quality or the identity of the investors selling domestic equities. The observed change is selective fund demand, not a complete map of global savings. [ICI, 14 October](https://www.ici.org/research/stats/combined_flows).

Money-market fund assets fell from **$7,963.55 billion** on 7 October to **$7,923.08 billion** on 14 October. Institutional funds accounted for **$39.38 billion** of the decline. The earlier week's sharp increase followed by a partial reversal undermines any interpretation based on a single weekly change. Money-market assets are a stock, mainly influenced by net subscriptions, and cannot be treated as the world's available cash. [ICI, 15 October](https://www.ici.org/research/stats/mmf).

## Client allocations through major managers are not proprietary capital

BlackRock reported **$205 billion of net client inflows in Q3**, **$869 billion over twelve months**, and **$15.5 trillion of assets under management** on 30 September. The quarterly flow shows client demand for the manager's products; AUM is a stock that also reflects market prices. Neither establishes beneficial ownership of those assets by BlackRock. The firm identified demand across ETFs, active equity, private markets and cash. Its client flows can overlap with ICI fund statistics and must not be added to them. [BlackRock, 14 October](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/newsroom/media/press-releases/blackrock-reports-third-quarter-2026).

The IMF's October financial-stability assessment identifies concentrated AI exposures, leverage, sovereign-yield shocks and operational dependencies as possible channels through which asset repricing can amplify funding stress. These are vulnerabilities, not evidence of a crisis this week. Published house views are distinct from observed client flows or proprietary positions. [IMF, October GFSR](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/GFSR/Issues/2026/10/13/global-financial-stability-report-october-2026).

## Physical gold holdings rose while their dollar valuation fell

September remains the latest full-month global gold-ETF observation. The World Gold Council reports **$10 billion of inflows**, **67 tonnes added** and **4,256 tonnes held**, while ETF assets under management declined **7% month on month to $574 billion** as gold prices fell. Q3 inflows of **$31 billion** include September; the periods are not additive. Rising quantities and falling asset values can coexist. Gold ETFs are private portfolios, not monetary-authority reserves; the IMF's COFER covers official foreign-exchange assets and excludes gold. [WGC, 7 October](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/data/global-gold-backed-etf-holdings-and-flows).

```flow
Foreign long-term securities purchases rise → August TIC inflow rebounds → long-term funding demand improves; ultimate owners remain uncertain
Foreign Treasury-bill holdings and bank dollar liabilities fall → short-term dollar exposure contracts → maturity composition diverges
Domestic-equity fund withdrawals + world-equity and bond subscriptions → selective fund demand → country destinations remain unidentified
Gold ETF subscriptions + falling gold price → tonnes held rise while AUM falls → quantity differs from valuation
```

## The next releases will test whether the change persists

| Verification | Release or period | Test |
|---|---|---|
| ICI long-term and money-market funds | October weeks | Persistence of bond/world-equity demand |
| Treasury TIC | September, scheduled 18 November | Whether August's inflow continues |
| BIS banking statistics | Next release scheduled 30 October | Cross-border bank credit versus securities flows |
| IMF COFER | Next quarterly update | Currency shares after valuation effects |
| WGC gold ETF data | October | Subscriptions, physical holdings and AUM separately |
| Corporate and public investment disclosures | Ongoing | Actual capex, commissioned facilities and ownership |

A lasting redistribution of productive capacity would require verified greenfield spending, operational factories, infrastructure utilisation, asset ownership or enforceable control rights. Announced data-centre investment and sovereign-fund commitments are not commissioned capacity. The central alternative to the present interpretation is a renewed sovereign-yield shock that causes simultaneous fund redemptions, tighter credit and weaker primary issuance. Conversely, persistent foreign long-term purchases and orderly credit markets would strengthen the selective-demand interpretation. These are testable conditions, not investment recommendations.
