# Global Monetary & Financial Institutions

## Scope

This controlled series tracks public institutions whose decisions can materially alter monetary conditions, financial stability, sovereign financing, cross-border liquidity, reserve management, development finance or the infrastructure through which money and securities settle.

It belongs to **Political Economy & Markets** and does not create a fifth permanent research program.

### Central banks and monetary authorities

Coverage is global. The monitored universe includes sovereign central banks, currency-area central banks and monetary authorities, including institutions outside the BIS membership where their decisions are material to domestic or cross-border conditions.

High-frequency attention is given to institutions with large currencies, financial systems, reserve positions or cross-border transmission, including the Federal Reserve, ECB/Eurosystem, People's Bank of China, Bank of Japan, Bank of England, Reserve Bank of India, Banco Central do Brasil, Bank of Canada, Reserve Bank of Australia, Swiss National Bank, Bank of Korea, Bank Indonesia, Banco de México, South African Reserve Bank and major regional central banks and currency unions.

### Multilateral financial institutions

Coverage includes the IMF; World Bank Group institutions; New Development Bank; Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank; Asian Development Bank; African Development Bank; Inter-American Development Bank; European Bank for Reconstruction and Development; CAF; Islamic Development Bank; and other multilateral lenders when a decision changes financing capacity, lending conditions, debt treatment or institutional architecture.

### Standard setters and financial-stability institutions

Coverage includes the BIS, Financial Stability Board, Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures, IOSCO and IAIS, plus relevant joint bodies and official working groups when their decisions affect prudential standards, payments, market infrastructure or systemic-risk oversight.

## What is monitored

The series records material changes in:

- policy rates, corridors, reserve remuneration and standing facilities;
- asset purchases, balance-sheet policy, quantitative tightening and reserve supply;
- foreign-exchange intervention, swap lines and emergency liquidity;
- reserve composition, gold policy and official foreign assets when disclosed;
- macroprudential rules, bank capital/liquidity standards and financial-stability frameworks;
- sovereign-debt assessment, restructuring and debt-sustainability frameworks;
- multilateral lending capacity, approved facilities, capital plans and material country programmes;
- local-currency financing and cross-border settlement arrangements;
- wholesale central-bank money, payment systems, CBDCs and tokenised settlement infrastructure;
- institutional mandates, governance rules and operational frameworks with material economic consequences.

Routine speeches, ceremonial meetings, small project announcements and repeated statements are collected only when they change the evidentiary record.

## Source hierarchy

For institutional decisions, primary sources are mandatory whenever available:

1. decision, communiqué, minutes, board paper or press release from the institution;
2. official statistical or operational data from the institution or relevant public authority;
3. BIS, IMF, World Bank or other official cross-institution datasets;
4. high-quality reporting for market reaction, context not available in the primary record, or confirmation of an event whose primary publication is temporarily inaccessible;
5. academic or specialist analysis for mechanisms and historical comparison.

Secondary reporting must not replace an accessible primary source for the terms of an official decision.

Decision date, meeting date, publication date and implementation date are kept distinct.

## Publication cadence

### Event-driven

Publish when a single decision has substantial structural or cross-border consequences. Examples include a material monetary-policy shift, emergency liquidity facility, major debt-framework revision, new settlement architecture, reserve-policy change or multilateral financing reform.

### Daily

Publish a concise institutional monitor only when at least one material change has occurred or several lower-intensity decisions form a meaningful common pattern. No publication is required merely to satisfy the calendar.

### Weekly

Synthesize the week across monetary stance, liquidity, reserves, debt, multilateral credit, regulation and financial infrastructure. The weekly edition should identify common transmission mechanisms and divergences without repeating the daily record.

### Monthly

Update slower-moving measures: balance sheets, reserve trends, official gold holdings where available, multilateral commitments, local-currency financing, payment infrastructure and changes in the global distribution of official liquidity and credit.

### Quarterly

Assess changes in the architecture of monetary and financial power: cross-border liquidity access, reserve-currency use, sovereign financing, financial-regulatory coordination, multilateral lending capacity and settlement infrastructure.

### Annual

Produce a documented state of the global monetary and financial architecture with comparable institutional data and a revision history.

## Materiality score

Each candidate receives a 0–100 materiality score. The score is a publication filter, not a ranking of institutions.

| Dimension | Maximum |
|---|---:|
| Monetary stance or system liquidity | 20 |
| Currency, reserves or cross-border liquidity | 15 |
| Sovereign debt or multilateral financing | 15 |
| Financial stability or prudential regulation | 15 |
| Payment and settlement infrastructure | 10 |
| Geographic and market reach | 10 |
| Structural novelty and persistence | 10 |
| Near-term implementation significance | 5 |

Guidance:

- **80–100:** event-driven publication is normally warranted.
- **50–79:** include in the daily monitor and weekly synthesis; publish separately only when the mechanism needs deeper treatment.
- **20–49:** retain for the weekly or monthly record if it contributes to a broader pattern.
- **0–19:** archive as monitoring input unless later evidence raises its significance.

No score can override source-quality or factual-QA failures.

## Analytical fields

For each material decision, record:

- institution;
- jurisdiction or constituency;
- decision date and effective date;
- instrument changed;
- previous and new setting where applicable;
- vote or governance mechanism where disclosed;
- stated rationale;
- direct transmission channel;
- cross-border transmission channel;
- affected balance sheets or markets;
- implementation horizon;
- reversibility;
- evidence that would weaken the interpretation;
- primary source;
- confidence level.

## Editorial standard

Public research states the external facts, mechanisms and uncertainties directly. It does not describe the publishing workflow, taxonomy, database, scoring implementation, renderer, QA process, internal tools or future editorial roadmap.

The following are blocking failures in public research:

- commentary about how a report, tool, map, dataset, series or system is being built or updated;
- phrases such as "in this report", "this analysis will", "the tool shows", "the system begins to", "in this first version", "what should be added next" or their Portuguese equivalents when used as process narration;
- references to prompts, agents, QA, staging, builds, commits, renderers, schemas or publication pipelines;
- conversational instructions to the reader;
- unsupported claims that an institution "controls", "changes the order" or "redistributes power" without naming the observable capability, asset, rule or transmission mechanism.

Substantive self-reference should be removed when the same sentence can state the evidence directly.

## QA gates

A publication cannot pass when any of the following is unresolved:

1. an official decision lacks a primary source that was available at review time;
2. a number lacks a date, unit, jurisdiction or adequate source context;
3. decision date and publication date are conflated;
4. fact, analysis and scenario are presented as the same epistemic category;
5. EN and PT-BR editions differ materially in figures, claims, visual evidence or confidence;
6. a causal statement skips an essential transmission step;
7. a chart or table contains values not supported in the accompanying sources;
8. public prose contains internal architecture, editorial instructions, QA language or conversational assistant-style text;
9. a repeated item is presented as new without a documented change;
10. the conclusion is stronger than the evidence available.

Publication failure should preserve the candidate for correction rather than publish degraded prose.
